## The register (7 lines)

Sources opened on 19 November 2026 unless stated.

- **Ministry of Finance, global sukuk, July 2026 ($1.5bn, issued; now under govt):** the Ministry’s release of 24 July 2026 (https://mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/malaysia-global-sukuk-240726) did not re-open. The Vibes, 24 July 2026: US$850 million of 5.75 years at “4.612%”, “a 15 basis-point spread”; US$650 million of 10 years at “4.949%”, “a 25 basis-point spread”; “the lowest spreads ever recorded for Malaysia’s global sukuk issuance”; “4.7 times” oversubscribed; proceeds for “general Shariah-compliant purposes, including financing development expenditure and refinancing existing obligations”. Bernama via New Straits Times, 4 November 2026: “received subscription demand exceeding US$9.5 billion (about RM38.1 billion)”.
- **Bank Negara Malaysia, SME Stabilisation Relief Facility (RM5bn, active):** “a RM5 billion financing facility”, “financing of up to RM750,000”, “a maximum financing rate of 3.75% per annum (inclusive of guarantee fee)”, “a tenure of up to five years”, for SMEs “including microenterprises, affected by the ongoing West Asia conflict”, providing “timely working capital support”. Bank Negara Malaysia, 28 April 2026, https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/sme-srf-bm . The Star, 9 October 2026: BNM “will allocate an additional RM5bil”; RM3.8 billion approved under the first allocation as at 30 September 2026; “The additional allocation is expected to benefit around another 9,000 SMEs, including microenterprises”, https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/10/09/bank-negara-allocates-additional-rm5bil-for-sme-relief-facility (news; the Bank’s own release of the additional allocation was not opened).
- **Bank Negara Malaysia, Fund for SMEs, additional allocation (RM2.5bn, active):** “an additional RM2.5 billion will be provided under BNM’s Fund”, focused on first-time borrowers; the fund rises to RM34.9 billion from RM32.4 billion of concessional financing made available through participating financial institutions; RM10 billion of guaranteed financing targeted with Credit Guarantee Corporation Malaysia Berhad. Bank Negara Malaysia, 6 January 2026, https://www.bnm.gov.my/en/-/addfund
- **Ministry of Finance, Sustainability Malaysian Government Investment Issue (RM4.5bn, issued):** “inaugural issuance” in the domestic market of the “Sustainability MGII” (MGII Lestari Sukuk), RM4.5 billion nominal, profit rate 4.662%, oversubscribed 2.38 times with bids of MYR10.7 billion; proceeds “will finance or refinance eligible social and green projects” under the “SDG Sukuk Framework”. Ministry of Finance, 30 September 2022, https://mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/mgii-sukuk
- **Ministry of Finance, sustainability sukuk, April 2021 ($1.3bn, issued):** upsized “to US$1.3 billion from US$1.0 billion initially”; 10-year trust certificates of US$800 million at 2.07% (T + 50 basis points) and 30-year of US$500 million at 3.075% (T + 80 basis points); oversubscribed 6.4 times; “the world’s first sovereign US Dollar Sustainability Sukuk”, under “The Government of Malaysia SDG Sukuk Framework”, assessed by Sustainalytics. Ministry of Finance, press citation of 22 April 2021, https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-citations/malaysia-s-sustainability-sukuk-oversubscribed-by-6-4-times-allocation-well-spread-globally
- **AIIB, Quadria Capital Fund III co-investment: Project Portofino ($4.8M, approved):** “Malaysia : Quadria Capital Fund III Co-investment: Project Portofino”, project “000690-CI02”, sector “Health Infrastructure”, “Nonsovereign”, “USD4.8 million”, approved “July 7, 2026”; a take-private acquisition of Apex Healthcare Berhad, “a Malaysia-headquartered pharmaceutical manufacturer and distributor”, to make affordable generic medicines more available across Southeast Asia. https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2026/approved/malaysia-quadria-capital-fund-iii-co-investment-project-portofino.html
- **IFC, cornerstone investment in Sunway Healthcare ($15M, invested):** “IFC has invested 60 million Malaysian Ringgit ($15 million) as a cornerstone investor in the IPO”; proceeds “will be used for the expansion of Sunway Healthcare’s hospital network in Malaysia”, with new hospitals in Seremban, Putrajaya and Iskandar Puteri and “a combined capacity of over 3,400 beds among its network of hospitals by 2032”. IFC, 18 March 2026, https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/world-bank-group-s-ifc-invests-in-sunway-healthcare-to-increase-access-to-healthca

## The enterprises

Opened 19 November 2026.

- **Langit Collective (Food):** “Langit Collective partners with smallholder farmers in East Malaysia to revive traditional rice-growing practices”; heirloom rice and crops sourced “directly from indigenous farmers”; farmers receive “at least 35% of retail proceeds”, and 30% of profits are reinvested in “capacity-building workshops”; AirAsia Foundation grant of “MYR 48,500”, February 2020 to January 2021. AirAsia Foundation, https://airasiafoundation.com/social_enterprise/langit-collective/ . Operating in 2024: “a social enterprise in Malaysia” working with indigenous farming communities in Sabah and Sarawak, including a Lun Bawang village in the highlands of Lawas; products sold in retail outlets across Malaysia and online (FedEx Business Insights, first published 31 October 2023, updated 8 August 2024, https://www.fedex.com/en-my/business-insights/sme/how-a-social-enterprise-revitalized-rural-economies-in-borneo.html). “The farmers are paid more than twice what they would get in the local markets” (Greenpeace, 21 October 2021, https://www.greenpeace.org/international/story/50065/better-food-systems-championing-heirloom-rice/).
- **Pandai (Education):** the Ministry of Entrepreneur Development and Cooperatives “accredited Pandai as a social enterprise”; “a personalised and interactive application”; paid “Pandai Premium”, with scholarships for students who “could not afford Pandai Premium”; company partners provide devices, data plans and training; programmes include Pandai @ Hope Worldwide Malaysia (PPR communities in three states) and the Albukhary Tuition Program (300 SPM students). Pandai, undated, https://my.pandai.org/corporate
- **MYReaders (Education):** “a community-driven reading programme”, with a “structured English reading intervention” through its Literacy Toolkit, “small and one-to-one reading groups”, teacher training and Orang Asli parent mentors (Juhuk Penanei); it has “impacted over 36,000 students, 2,000 parents, and 1,100 teachers in 144 schools and 67 communities across the country”. Free Malaysia Today, 10 February 2025, https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2025/02/10/how-myreaders-is-closing-literacy-gaps-across-malaysia . Earned income: “Our current source of revenue is garnered from sales of our literacy toolkits, government grants and partnerships”; accredited as a social enterprise by the Ministry of Entrepreneur Development and MaGIC in 2020 (MIT Solve, last updated 17 October 2021, https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/53119).
- **EPIC Homes (Shelter):** “EPIC (Extraordinary People Impacting Communities) is a multiple award-winning social enterprise”; “EPIC Homes team building programmes foster empathy, leadership, and cross-functional collaboration”; it has helped “provide safe homes for over 350 families in need” among “the indigenous Orang Asli of Peninsular Malaysia” and “mobilised 9,000+ individuals and 200+ organisations” since 2010. MSIG Malaysia release via Malay Mail, 2 October 2026, https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/mediaoutreach/2026/10/02/msig-malaysia-reinforces-long-term-commitment-to-community-resilience-through-partnership-with-epic-homes/491486
- **PichaEats (Livelihood):** “a Malaysian social enterprise founded in 2016 that empowers refugee families through food”, creating “sustainable catering and culinary opportunities”, “turning home-cooked meals into sustainable income opportunities”; it has “partnered with over 40 refugee chefs”, “served more than 1,000 clients” and “channelled back over MYR 7 million to the community”. Asia Food Journal, 26 August 2025, https://asiafoodjournal.com/?p=15431 . UNHCR Malaysia (30 June 2020): “a Malaysian social enterprise that promotes and markets food from the refugee community in Malaysia”, https://unhcr.org/my/node/26343
- **Batik Boutique (Livelihood):** “an award-winning, globally recognised social enterprise”, the nation’s only B Corp-certified batik brand, selling handcrafted batik apparel; it “employs around 400 artisans” and works with “low-income women, refugees and artisans from marginalised communities”; “over 20,000 handcrafted pieces” in 2025; four stores in the Klang Valley. Free Malaysia Today, 8 March 2026, https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2026/03/08/the-american-building-livelihoods-with-malaysian-batik . Named among the ten social enterprises of the Social Enterprise Accelerator Malaysia (Biji-biji Initiative and IKEA Social Entrepreneurship), Warga Biz, 23 January 2026, https://www.wargabiz.com.my/2026/01/23/seam-closing-summit-2026-spotlights-10-social-enterprises/
- **The Asli Co. (Livelihood):** “a social enterprise that helps underprivileged women”, selling pots, soaps, face masks, sanitisers, tote bags and eye pillows made at home by Orang Asli women in Kampung Bukit Serok (Pahang) and Kampung Orang Asli Serendah, Hulu Kuang and Desa Temuan (Selangor); “The workers make RM600 a month on average”; job opportunities for “more than 60 indigenous mothers from eight villages”; over RM1.1 million disbursed for raw materials, equipment and wages. The Edge Malaysia, 23 January 2023, via Options, https://www.optionstheedge.com/node/3211 . Still operating in 2026: one of the ten social enterprises of the Social Enterprise Accelerator Malaysia, whose “handmade products by B40 women, single mothers and Orang Asli” were selected “as Istana Negara souvenirs” (Warga Biz, 23 January 2026, https://www.wargabiz.com.my/2026/01/23/seam-closing-summit-2026-spotlights-10-social-enterprises/).
