## The seven standards

Not re-read at source on 23 November 2026: the World Bank API did not open, and no newer primary release of the same indicators was found.

- **Food, Not measured:** No FAO measure of food insecurity is published for Iraq. FAO, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?locations=IQ
- **Water, 59.8%:** Safely managed drinking water. WHO/UNICEF, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?locations=IQ
- **Shelter, 50.7%:** City dwellers outside slums; 49.3% live in slums. UN-Habitat, 2022, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EN.POP.SLUM.UR.ZS?locations=IQ
- **Energy, 44.7%:** The grid produced 24,600 MW in September 2026, against the 55,000 to 60,000 MW needed for power around the clock. 99% cook with clean fuels (2024). Ministry of Electricity, Sep 2026, https://www.iraqinews.com/iraq/iraqs-electricity-shortage-surpasses-35000-megawatts/
- **Education, 44.9:** 44.9% of young people complete lower secondary school; 2007, dated. World Bank, 2007, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.SEC.CMPT.LO.ZS?locations=IQ
- **Health, 64:** WHO service coverage index. WHO, 2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~IRQ
- **Livelihoods, 72%:** Above US$8.30 a day; 28% live below it. World Bank, 2023, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.UMIC?locations=IQ

Furthest from reach: Energy (55.3%). Of the electricity Iraq needs is not supplied: 24,600 MW against at least 55,000 MW, September 2026.

- **Energy, delivery test (12 November 2026):** Production 24,600 MW against 55,000 to 60,000 MW needed for 24-hour supply (Ahmed Turki Jiyad, spokesperson of the Ministry of Electricity, IraqiNews, 20 September 2026; the ministry released 24,700 MW for September on 14 September 2026). 24,600 / 55,000 = 44.7%, the lower demand figure, so the gap is not overstated. Peak-season reading. Energy sets Coverage.

## The register (6 lines)

Each line links its own source. Opened on 23 November 2026.

- **Economic stimulus financing package, IQD 3.5 trillion (new):** Arab News, “Iraq approves $2.7bn financing package to stimulate economy”, 5 October 2026: “Iraq has approved a 3.5 trillion dinars ($2.7 billion) financing package”, directed by Prime Minister Ali Faleh Al-Zaidi at a meeting he chaired on 4 October; 1 trillion dinars from existing Central Bank of Iraq initiatives to the real estate and housing sector; at least 1 trillion dinars of additional liquidity for the Trade Bank of Iraq and the Industrial Bank for industrial projects; 500 billion dinars for contractors’ dues; 500 billion dinars for farmers’ dues; 500 billion dinars more for the Generations Fund, bringing its balance to 1.5 trillion dinars. Arab News credits “the Iraqi News Agency as reported by Asharq Bloomberg”, https://www.arabnews.pk/business/iraq-approves-27bn-financing-package-to-stimulate-economy-3003229 . Iraq Business News, 6 October 2026, reports the same measures (“around IQD 3.5 trillion ($2.7bn)”; housing IQD 1 trillion, $763m), from the Prime Minister’s Office, https://www.iraq-businessnews.com/2026/10/06/iraq-launches-2-7bn-economic-stimulus-package/ . No page of the Prime Minister’s Office, the Ministry of Finance or the Central Bank of Iraq was opened: news sources only.
- **World Bank, Iraq Transport Economic Corridors (ITREC), $900 million (refreshed):** see the hero. The release names the “Iraq Transport Economic Corridors (ITREC) project”; it reaches “approximately 7.9 million people living along new or improved roadways”, “including around 3.0 million women and 1.5 million youth”; “the sector is an important source of employment, with road construction and rehabilitation generating jobs”; the roads “connect people to markets and services”. The release does not name the World Bank lending arm.
- **Green Climate Fund, SAP060, USD 20.2 million (new):** GCF project page, “Promoting Climate Resilient Livelihoods for Food Insecure People in Southern Iraq”: accredited entity World Food Programme; approved “30 Oct 2025”; FAA effective “23 Sep 2026”; estimated completion “23 Sep 2030”; GCF financing “USD 20,221,682”, a grant; total value “$ 22.3 M million”; “801 K thousand” people with increased resilience; status “Under implementation”, https://www.greenclimate.fund/project/sap060 . - **World Bank, Pollution Management Project, $18.5 million (refreshed):** World Bank, Washington, 25 February 2025: “The World Bank Board of Executive Directors approved a US$18.5 million project”, financed by “a US$13.49 million grant from the Global Environment Facility (GEF)” and “a US$5 million grant from the Iraq Reform, Recovery and Reconstruction Fund (I3RF)”; it “aims to destroy, dispose of or contain about 4,000 tons of POPs/ hazardous chemicals”, “reducing health risks affecting about seven million people”, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/02/20/iraq-pollution-management-project-to-improve-health-and-livelihoods-of-communities

## The enterprises

Opened 23 November 2026.

- **Al Jabal Agriculture (Food):** Wamda, 9 June 2026: an Iraq-based company that “provides AI-powered smart farming solutions using satellite monitoring, GIS technologies, and agricultural analytics”, founded in 2022 by Mohammed Nabeel Al-Ruwaishdi; it manages thousands of dunams across Iraq and reports productivity gains above 25% for several clients; a six-figure investment from Iraqi investors, https://www.wamda.com/en/2026/06/mabiati-al-jabal-agriculture-raise-figure-investments-iraqi-investors . Orange Corners, 4 June 2026: “integrated smart farming services”; “thousands of dunams across Iraq”; part of “Orange Corners Baghdad Cohort 10”, https://www.orangecorners.com/al-jabal-agriculture-secures-six-figure-investment-to-expand-smart-farming-solutions-across-iraq/
- **Ras Al-Ain Sky (Energy):** Water and Energy for Food (WE4F), innovator profile, 3 October 2024: “Financial services that help farmers access agricultural inputs and technologies”; solar water pumps, solar irrigation systems and inverters, with payment facilities and microloans of US$3,000 to US$5,000 at 8% interest, repaid quarterly, so that smallholder farmers can buy them; “Stage 3: 1,001 to 10,000 end-users”; incorporated and operating in Iraq, https://we4f.org/innovators/ras-al-ain-sky . A winner of the second WE4F Iraq Call for Innovations, 14 August 2024, https://we4f.org/program-news/iraq-cfi-winners-2024
- **KESK (Energy):** Wilson Center, Middle East Women’s Initiative, 20 April 2023, by the founder and CEO, Basima Abdulrahman: KESK, founded in October 2018; “our solar energy solutions allow clients to shift to a more sustainable and reliable source of energy”; “our annual installation capacity has reached 1000 KWP”, https://wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/green-building-addresses-energy-crisis-iraq . PwC Middle East, Future50 profile (undated): “KESK aims to make smart solar energy solutions trustable, accessible, and profitable for businesses in Iraq”; it “designs, supplies, and instals smart solar-powered air conditioning systems”; a Cartier Women’s Initiative award of US$100,000 in 2021, https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/esg/images/future50/docs/energy/KESK.pdf
- **Kasho Company (Livelihood):** WE4F, innovator profile, 3 October 2024: “High-quality sunflower oil gained through cold-press technology”; “Kasho Company focuses on sunflower seed cultivation, processing by utilizing cold-press technology”; it supplies farmers with sunflower seeds and buys their harvest; “Stage 5: 1 million+ end-users”; incorporated and operating in Iraq, https://we4f.org/innovators/kasho-company . WE4F Iraq winners, 14 August 2024: “Provides smallholder farmers with sunflower seeds, helping them use their crops to produce high-quality sunflower oil”.
- **Vitas Iraq (Livelihood):** Proparco, 4 August 2022: a “USD 3.95 million portfolio guarantee” with Vitas Iraq, part of Vitas Group, a microfinance institution “committed to bridging the gap to the financial inclusion of MSMEs”, “With over 375,000 historic clients, 410,000 disbursed loans & 20,000 jobs created”, https://proparco.fr/en/actualites/proparco-and-vitas-group-join-efforts-support-small-entrepreneurs-iraq-new-partnership . IOM, Access2Finance overview, January 2025, lists VITAS Iraq as a partner: IOM supported “transitioning CHF-Vitas from a non-profit organization to a for-profit entity regulated by the Central Bank of Iraq” and “piloted a green energy financing project that promoted renewable energy adoption among SMEs in Baghdad and Basra”, https://edf.iom.int//publications/540/Access2Finance_Overview_EN_Jan25_V04_compressed.pdf
