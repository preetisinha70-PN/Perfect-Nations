## The seven standards

Not re-read on 26 November 2026; the figures below stand as published.

- **Food, 27%:** Not food insecure; 73% are moderately or severely food insecure. FAO, 2023, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?locations=YE
- **Water, 75%:** Basic drinking water; safely managed water is not measured (lenient). WHO/UNICEF, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.BASW.ZS?locations=YE
- **Shelter, 43.1%:** Safely managed sanitation; 44.2% of city dwellers live in slums. WHO/UNICEF, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.STA.SMSS.ZS?locations=YE
- **Energy, 12.9:** About 129 kWh of electricity a person a year, against the Living Line of 1,000; 47% cook with clean fuels (2024). Ember, 2024, https://ember-energy.org/data/yearly-electricity-data/
- **Education, 5.3:** 5.3% of children read a simple text by the end of primary school; 2011, dated. World Bank, 2011, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?locations=YE
- **Health, 43:** WHO service coverage index. WHO, 2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~YEM
- **Livelihoods, 51.4%:** Above the national poverty line; 48.6% live below it, dated. World Bank, 2014, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.NAHC?locations=YE

Furthest from reach: Education (94.7%). Of children in Yemen cannot read a simple text by the end of primary school, on 2011 data.

## Capital already committed (the capital instruments register)

Thirteen lines, in the order govt, humanitarian, multilateral, regional, bilateral, private, impact; newest first within each. Opened 26 November 2026 unless stated.

### Humanitarian

- **EU humanitarian funding for Yemen, 2026 (€110M):** The Brussels Times, 23 October 2026, reporting a European Commission release, https://www.brusselstimes.com/2374993/eu-commits-e22-5m-to-yemen-as-conflict-leaves-millions-in-crisis. “an additional €22.5 million in humanitarian funding for Yemen”, “bringing its planned EU humanitarian support for 2026 to €110 million”.
- **WFP emergency food assistance in Yemen ($268M):** World Food Programme, Yemen emergency page (undated), https://www.wfp.org/emergencies/yemen-emergency. “WFP urgently requires US$268 million” to cover operations “up to March 2027”; “WFP continues to support 1.7 million vulnerable people in Government-controlled areas”.
- **Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan 2026 ($2.16B):** United Nations and humanitarian partners, release of 18 March 2026 (ReliefWeb, OCHA), https://reliefweb.int/report/yemen/yemen-humanitarian-crisis-deepens-aid-agencies-appeal-us216-billion-assist-millions-2026-enar. “US$2.16 billion”; “22.3 million people require humanitarian assistance and protection services”; partners “aim to reach 12 million people”.
- **UNICEF Humanitarian Action for Children, Yemen 2026 ($146.3M):** UNICEF, Yemen appeal (March 2026), https://www.unicef.org/appeals/yemen. “UNICEF is appealing for US$146.3 million”; services to “5.2 million people, including 3.5 million children”.

## The enterprises

Opened 26 November 2026.

- **Solar Care (Energy):** UNDP, “Made in Yemen: Youth innovate with local solutions built to last”, 12 August 2025, https://www.undp.org/arab-states/stories/made-yemen-youth-innovate-local-solutions-built-last. “a solar panel cleaning and maintenance service” using dry-cleaning tools instead of water; the team can clean up to six hundred panels in four hours.
- **Dorri (Education):** same UNDP story, 12 August 2025. Sensory learning toys of fabric and wood: “Today, Dorri’s products are used in dozens of kindergartens.”
- **Al-Amal Microfinance Bank (Livelihood):** Al-Amal Microfinance Bank, news of 26 May 2025, https://alamalbank.com/en/news/16. “serving over 330,000 clients, including 35% women”; a Green Finance pillar for “solar energy, climate-smart agriculture, and water conservation”. A February 2026 study by the bank on FinDev Gateway (https://www.findevgateway.org/paper/2026/02/from-resilience-to-sustainable-recovery) describes its “Twin Modality” model and green finance products.
- **ONE Cash (Livelihood):** Forbes Middle East, The Middle East’s Fintech 50 2026 (No. 28), https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50-2026/one-cash/. “a digital wallet enabling individuals and businesses in Yemen to pay, receive, and transfer money”; more than 850,000 active customers and about 80,000 merchants; transactions up 32% in 2025 to around $3.3 billion. Owned by the Hayel Saeed Anam Group.
- **Jawali, operated by WeCash (Livelihood):** The Fintech Times, 10 September 2026, https://thefintechtimes.com/yemen-can-fintech-connect-an-economy-divided-by-war/. “Another example is Jawali, operated by WeCash”: transfers, salaries and aid payments, purchases and bills without a conventional bank account.
- **Qima Coffee (Livelihood):** World Coffee Portal (5THWAVE, 2024), https://worldcoffeeportal.com/Latest/InsightAnalysis/2024/December/The-partnership-bringing-Yemen-s-prized-specialty. Yemen’s largest exporter of specialty coffee; farmer partners receive on average over 400% above the standard international market price; its processing centre has benefited more than 1,500 smallholder producers. Utrecht University, 18 August 2026 (https://www.uu.nl/en/news/brewing-sustainability-in-yemeni-coffee), confirms current work with “Qima Coffee, an international ethical coffee sourcing, marketing, and development organization”.

Searched on 26 November 2026: UNDP stories on solar microgrids and women’s solar businesses (no single named operator), solar water and rural clinics (no named organisation), mobile wallets in Yemen, specialty coffee, Yemeni health and education start-ups (MIT Solve, GEN), IFC investments in Yemen, and microfinance. Not listed: a portable-ultrasound health programme on MIT Solve (headquartered outside Yemen, grant-funded, no year shown) and an online doctor-booking platform (no date from 2024 or later).
