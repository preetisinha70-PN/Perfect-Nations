## Sources of the figures that carry no link of their own

### The seven standards
- **Food, 23.5%:** share not moderately or severely food insecure; 76.5% are moderately or severely food insecure (FAO, 2024, via the World Bank indicator SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS).
- **Water, 29.9%:** safely managed drinking water (WHO/UNICEF, 2024, indicator SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS).
- **Shelter, 32.2%:** safely managed sanitation (WHO/UNICEF, 2024, indicator SH.STA.SMSS.ZS).
- **Energy, 16.5:** electricity demand per person of about 165 kWh in 2025 against the Living Line of 1,000 kWh (Ember, Yearly Electricity Data, 2026 release, https://ember-energy.org/data/yearly-electricity-data/, read through the Our World in Data energy dataset on 12 November 2026); 28% cook with clean fuels (2024). 165 / 1,000 = 16.5%. Energy sets Coverage.
- **Education:** not measured; neither children reading by the end of primary school nor lower-secondary completion is published for Nigeria.
- **Health, 47:** WHO service coverage index, 2023 (via Our World in Data).
- **Livelihood, 36%:** share above US$4.20 a day; 64% live below it (World Bank, 2022, indicator SI.POV.LMIC).
- None of the seven was re-read on 26 November 2026: the World Bank data pages were not opened in this review.

### The Sovereign Assets
- **The People, 237.5m, 2025; 64 in every hundred in towns and cities:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, population and urban share, read 30 October 2026; not re-read.
- **The Land, 77.1% of land in farms and pasture; The Forest, 18.9% of land under forest; The Water, 990 m³ of renewable fresh water per person; The Power, 22%:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, read 30 October 2026; not re-read.
- **The Power, label:** the card reads “of energy from renewables, 2021”. IRENA’s energy profile for Nigeria (updated 31 July 2024, https://www.irena.org/IRENADocuments/Statistical_Profiles/Africa/Nigeria_Africa_RE_SP.pdf) gives renewables at 75% of total energy supply in 2021 (mostly biomass) and 27% of electricity generation in 2022 (9,447 GWh of 34,780 GWh). The 22% is therefore not a share of energy; it is most likely the World Bank’s renewable electricity output share for 2021, to be confirmed.
