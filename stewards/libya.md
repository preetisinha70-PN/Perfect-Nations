## The hero

- **1.49M bpd, oil and condensate output, 21 June 2026:** 1,487,723 barrels a day on 21 June 2026, the highest since 2013, as reported by the National Oil Corporation (NOC). Rio Times, Libya oil economy 2026, citing the NOC, https://www.riotimesonline.com/libya-oil-economy-2026/ (read for the first edition, 10 November 2026). The World Bank gives oil production “averaging 1.3 mbpd by mid-2026” (World Bank, Libya country page, https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/libya, opened 17 November 2026): an average, not a single day.
- **$30bn, first unified budget in 13 years, April 2026:** “The HoR and the HCS agreed in April 2026 to Libya’s first unified state budget since 2013 — worth 190 billion dinars (approximately US$30 billion, or 76 percent of GDP).” World Bank, Libya country page (page modified 2 November 2026), https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/libya, opened 17 November 2026. Earlier news reports gave US$29.95 billion and 11 April 2026 (Rio Times; The National, 22 April 2026).
- **$3.26bn, oil revenue to the sovereign account, June 2026:** US$3,260,938,421 transferred to the sovereign account at the Libyan Foreign Bank in June 2026, as disclosed by the NOC. North Africa Post, 2 July 2026, https://northafricapost.com/?p=98576 (read for the first edition).

## The record and the register

### The record (5 lines)

- **$30bn:** Libya’s first unified state budget since 2013, 190 billion dinars, about 76% of GDP, agreed in April 2026 by the House of Representatives and the High Council of State. World Bank, Libya country page, https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/libya (opened 17 November 2026)
- **146%:** “Public debt rose to 146 percent of GDP over the past two years.” “Fiscal deficits reached about 30 percent of GDP in 2025.” “Energy subsidies are about 20 percent of GDP and the wage bill is around 30 percent of GDP.” IMF, Libya: Staff Concluding Statement of the 2026 Article IV Consultation Mission (discussions in Tunis, 30 March to 8 April 2026; published 10 April 2026), https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/10/mcs-04102026-libya-staff-concluding-statement-of-the-2026-article-iv-consultation-mission (opened 17 November 2026)
- **$3.26bn:** Oil revenue transferred to the sovereign account at the Libyan Foreign Bank in June 2026, as disclosed by the NOC. North Africa Post, 2 July 2026, https://northafricapost.com/?p=98576
- **$8bn:** “public spending depleted foreign reserves by USD 8 billion annually during 2023-2025”; consumer prices up 11.1% year on year in the first half of 2026. World Bank, Libya country page, https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/libya (search result read 25 November 2026). Added as the reserves line, because neither the IMF nor the Central Bank of Libya publishes a current reserves total that could be read here.
- **$2bn+:** Operating budget for the National Oil Corporation within the unified budget (about LYD 12 to 13 billion). The National, 22 April 2026, https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/04/22/libya-hits-record-output-and-approves-first-unified-budget-in-13-years/

## The enterprises

- **REC (Shelter):** a recycling enterprise in Derna; collects and recycles debris from demolished buildings, damaged trees and organic waste; resumed operations with the Ruwwad Project after Storm Daniel (September 2023). UNDP Libya, “World MSME Day: Local Solutions Anchoring Economic Growth Across Libya”, 27 June 2026, https://www.undp.org/libya/stories/world-msme-day-local-solutions-anchoring-economic-growth-across-libya
- **Panda (Education):** launched as School Connect, one of the top 12 winners of the Tatweer Research Impact Fund; “Today, Panda connects over 10,000 students and their families to thirty schools”; part of Avanin Technology Education. Tatweer Research, economic empowerment (undated page), https://tatweerresearch.org/economic-empowerment
- **Speetar (Health):** a telehealth platform headquartered in Libya; “724,830 unique individuals have engaged with Speetar’s digital health services” (across its countries of work: Libya, Jordan, Egypt, Tunisia, the United States and Somalia). MIT Solve, https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/60245. Also listed as a Humanitarian Grand Challenge innovator, implementation country Libya, at the Transition to Scale stage (5 November 2024), https://humanitariangrandchallenge.org/innovator/speetar-inc/
- **Al Reaya Al Tebeya for Medical Services (Health):** Benghazi, founded in 2023 by a medical doctor; home-based healthcare; supported by the EEYES project (financed by the African Development Bank’s Youth Entrepreneurship and Innovation Multi-Donor Trust Fund, implemented by UNDP). African Development Bank, “In Libya, Young Entrepreneurs Turn Resilience Into New Businesses”, 15 May 2026, as published by allAfrica, https://allafrica.com/stories/202605180726.html
- **Tamouh (Livelihood):** Sebha; an all-female service for device repair and digital maintenance, founded by a team of female technicians; IT jobs for female engineering graduates. UNDP Libya, 27 June 2026, https://www.undp.org/libya/stories/world-msme-day-local-solutions-anchoring-economic-growth-across-libya
- **Kayan (Livelihood):** Benghazi; a digital platform for wholesale procurement connecting suppliers, traders and business owners; jobs in warehousing, logistics, sourcing and delivery. UNDP Libya, 27 June 2026, same URL.
- **Infinity Libya (Energy):** a subsidiary of Infinity Power; with the Al-Jouf Free Zone, completed what is described as Libya’s first 1 MW solar plant, in Kufra, handed over on 25 May 2025. SolarQuarter, 29 July 2025, https://solarquarter.com/2025/07/29/libya-commissions-its-first-1-mw-solar-power-plant-in-kufra-developed-by-infinity-libya-and-al-jouf-free-zone/ (business press).
