## The seven standards

Not re-read at source on 19 November 2026.

- **Food, Not measured:** No FAO measure of food insecurity is published for North Korea. FAO, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?locations=KP
- **Water, 66.6%:** Safely managed drinking water. WHO/UNICEF, 2023, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?locations=KP
- **Shelter, 77%:** City dwellers outside slums; 23% live in slums; safely managed sanitation is not measured. UN-Habitat, 2022, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EN.POP.SLUM.UR.ZS?locations=KP
- **Energy, 22%:** 22% cook with clean fuels and technology; 61.4% have electricity. World Bank, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.CFT.ACCS.ZS?locations=KP
- **Education, Not measured:** Neither learning outcomes nor lower-secondary completion is published for North Korea. World Bank, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.SEC.CMPT.LO.ZS?locations=KP
- **Health, 77:** WHO service coverage index. WHO, 2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~PRK
- **Livelihoods, Not measured:** No poverty survey for North Korea is published by the World Bank. World Bank, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.DDAY?locations=KP

Furthest from reach: Energy (78%). Of people in the DPRK still cook without clean fuels.

## The register (3 lines)

Sources opened on 19 November 2026.

- **UNICEF with Gavi, vaccines for routine immunisation, 2025 (delivered; no amount stated):** United Nations in the DPRK, “2025 Immunization Program”, 19 December 2025: “In 2025, UNICEF arranged six chartered flights to deliver 3.3 million vaccine doses to the DPRK”; hepatitis B, measles-rubella, pentavalent and inactivated poliovirus vaccines; “99 per cent of the birth cohorts from 2022 to 2025 who had missed their full routine vaccinations were fully immunized”; “UNICEF also procured and installed many cold chain equipment”; “These achievements were made possible through ongoing efforts by Gavi and UNICEF to keep the vaccines accessible”; “Vaccines were administered by national implementation partners”, https://dprk.un.org/en/309984-2025-immunization-program . The resumption of supplies: UNICEF press release of 18 July 2024, “On July 15, 16 and 18, 2024, three vaccine shipments arrived in DPRK”, “More than four million doses”, “in partnership with Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance (Gavi) and the World Health Organization (WHO)”; “The resumption of vaccine supplies is a critical step towards reestablishing the national immunization program”; UNICEF urged “the earliest possible return of UNICEF and all UN international staff”, https://dprk.un.org/en/275251-life-saving-vaccines-children-and-pregnant-women-delivered-democratic-people%E2%80%99s-republic
- **UNICEF, country programme for the DPRK (extended to December 2026; no budget stated):** UNICEF Executive Board, second regular session 2025, “Extensions of ongoing country programmes”, E/ICEF/2025/P/L.25/Rev.1, 4 September 2025: original programme period “2017–2025 (including four consecutive one-year extensions)”; extension “January to December 2026 (one-year extension)”, “the fifth consecutive extension”; “This one-year extension has been requested due to an extension of the United Nations Strategic Framework” for the DPRK, “following an agreement between the United Nations Resident Coordinator’s Office and the Government”, https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4087731/files/E_ICEF_2025_P_L.25_Rev.1-EN.pdf . The previous extension, to December 2025, “due to the extension of the United Nations Strategic Framework” (UNICEF Executive Board, 2024), https://Unicef.org/executiveboard/media/21326/file/2024-PL8-Extensions-of-CPs-EN-ODS-13-05-24.pdf
- **World Food Programme, interim country strategic plan 2019 to 2025 ($285.1M; plan period ended 31 December 2025):** “Democratic People’s Republic of Korea interim country strategic plan, revision 04”, issued 8 July 2024: “The purpose of this budget revision is to extend” the plan to 31 December 2025; total cost from USD 247,717,223 to USD 285,058,941; 803,134 beneficiaries added, 6,025,858 in all; “The last international personnel for WFP and the United Nations left the country in March 2021”; “there has been no WFP or United Nations international presence in the country”; “no distribution of WFP assistance” since; nutrition support for “children in kindergartens, nurseries, boarding schools” and “pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls”, “local food production and fortification”, “disaster risk reduction”, https://docs.wfp.org/api/documents/WFP-0000160131/download . WFP Executive Board, 24 January 2025: revision 4 “extends the 2024 needs-based plan into 2025” and the plan “until 31 December 2025”, increasing “the overall budget by USD 37.3 million”; “The last international personnel of WFP and the United Nations left the country in March 2021”, https://executiveboard.wfp.org/document_download/WFP-0000164043 . No later revision was found.
