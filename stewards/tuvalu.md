### Multilateral

- **World Bank, Second Climate and Disaster Resilience DPF ($7M, committed):** “FUNAFUTI, March 1, 2025”; “Tuvalu Second Climate and Disaster Resilience Development Policy Financing”; “US$7 million in new grants”; “the second in a series of two operations”; part of “coordinated budget support under the Government’s Policy Reform Matrix”. Disasters cost Tuvalu almost 7 percent of its GDP each year (World Bank Group, Pacific Atoll Countries Climate and Development Report, 2024, cited in the release). https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/03/03/new-world-bank-grant-to-enhance-tuvalu-s-climate-financing-disaster-response-and-resilience . ADB lists “a $7 million grant cofinancing from the World Bank for the Supporting Public Sector Management Reform Program, Subprogram 1” in 2025 (https://www.adb.org/where-we-work/tuvalu/overview).
- **World Bank, Maritime Investment in Climate Resilient Operations II, additional financing ($42M, approved 15 May 2024):** not re-read; the project page https://projects.worldbank.org/en/projects-operations/project-detail/P181595 did not open. A transport project, kept from the first edition.
- **World Bank, Pacific Islands Regional Oceanscape Program, second phase: Tuvalu ($13.1M, approved 8 December 2023):** press release “WASHINGTON, December 8, 2023”: “US$13 million grant”, funded through the “International Development Association (IDA)”, to “strengthen fisheries policies” and “boost sustainable community-based resource management and protection”, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/12/09/world-bank-supports-tuvalu-in-strengthening-fisheries-management . The project page P179599 did not open; US$13.1 million is the commitment read in the first edition.
- **World Bank, First Climate and Disaster Resilience DPF with a Cat DDO ($11.5M, approved 22 September 2023):** press release “WASHINGTON, September 26, 2023”: “US$11.5 million (AUD$18 million)”; a “development policy grant of US$7.5 million (AU$11.8 million)” and “an additional Cat DDO of US$4 million (AU$6.3 million)”, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/09/26/-world-bank-provides-us-11-5-million-grant-to-boost-tuvalu-s-climate-and-disaster-resilience-preparedness-and-response . The project page P179820 opened without its project data.
- **Green Climate Fund and UNDP, Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project, FP015 ($36.01M, under implementation; approved before 2024):** “Under implementation”; “Date approved” “30 Jun 2016”; “Total GCF-financing USD 36,010,000”; “Total co-financing USD 2,860,000”; “FAA effective” “07 Jun 2017”; “Completion date” “07 Dec 2025”; “The project will build coastal resilience in three of Tuvalu’s nine inhabited islands”; “2,780m of high-value vulnerable coastline will be protected.” The seven disbursements listed, from 30 June 2017 to 12 December 2024, add up to USD 36,010,000 (arithmetic). https://www.greenclimate.fund/project/fp015 . UNDP, 1 November 2025, “A Grandmother’s Fret and a Common Hope for Tuvalu’s future”: “the Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project Phase 2 (TCAP 1A)”; “eight hectares of new land have been reclaimed and raised on Fogafale”; designed “to remain above projected sea level rise beyond 2100”, https://www.undp.org/node/553496 . Australia: “Australia has committed $38 million to the Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project (TCAP)” (DFAT, https://dfat.gov.au/geo/tuvalu/development-assistance/development-assistance-in-tuvalu).

### Regional development bank

- **Asian Development Bank, Pacific Regional Energy Security Emergency Assistance Project ($11.94M for Tuvalu, approved):** “MANILA, PHILIPPINES (12 October 2026)”; “The Asian Development Bank (ADB) has approved $40.1 million to help Kiribati, Tonga, and Tuvalu”; “will provide grants of $12.77 million to Kiribati, $15.39 million to Tonga, and $11.94 million to Tuvalu”; “provide targeted assistance in Tuvalu to around 6,000 people from low-income households”. https://www.adb.org/news/amid-middle-east-crisis-adb-supports-energy-security-pacific-40-1-million
- **Asian Development Bank, Supporting Public Sector Management Reform Program ($7M, approved):** “MANILA, PHILIPPINES (11 November 2025)”; “$7 million policy-based grant”; “The Tuvalu Supporting Public Sector Management Reform Program”; grant from the Asian Development Fund; two subprograms, for fiscal resilience, the “transparency and accountability of public entities” and climate resilience, linked to the national goal of relying entirely on renewable energy by 2030; policy-based grants are paid into the government’s general budget once agreed reforms are completed. https://www.adb.org/news/adb-approves-7-million-grant-strengthen-tuvalu-fiscal-resilience-and-inclusive-growth . The release does not say which subprogram it finances.
- **Asian Development Bank, Funafuti Water Supply and Sanitation Project ($7.34M, signed):** signed in Funafuti on 19 November 2024; ADB grant $7.34 million from the Asian Development Fund; $4.59 million from the Global Environment Facility’s Least Developed Countries Fund; $4.3 million from New Zealand; $300,000 from the Government of Tuvalu; a new reticulated network for desalinated water, improved sanitation, upgraded school and health centre facilities and a sewage treatment plant; the network “will benefit more than half of the country’s population”. https://www.adb.org/news/adb-tuvalu-sign-grant-agreements-improve-water-and-sanitation-services-funafuti . The sources listed add up to $16.53 million (arithmetic). ADB environmental and social monitoring report, July 2026, January to June 2026: the project is in its Project Readiness Financing phase (“Grant-6301 TUV”); “installation of desalination plant” and the elevated reservoir are yet to commence, https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/project-documents/53417/53417-002-esmr-1164451-en.pdf . ADB’s Tuvalu page gives the GEF cofinancing as $4.6 million.
- **Asian Development Bank and GEF, batteries and electricity networks ($9M, committed 2024):** “In 2024, ADB committed another $9 million ADF grant, supplemented by a $1.84 million GEF grant”, “to replace endof-life batteries and upgrade damaged electricity networks in Tuvalu’s outer islands and Funafuti.” (linked to project 49450-015); “As of 31 December 2025, ADB had committed 50 public sector loans, grants, and technical assistance” “totaling $184 million to Tuvalu.” ADB, Tuvalu and ADB (fact sheet updated 19 May 2026), https://www.adb.org/where-we-work/tuvalu/overview . The project page did not open; its name and approval date are not yet read.
