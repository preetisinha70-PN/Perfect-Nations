## The seven standards

- **Food, 82.8%:** Not food insecure; 17.2% are moderately or severely food insecure. FAO, 2022, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?locations=BS
- **Water, 99%:** Basic drinking water; safely managed water is not measured (lenient), dated. WHO/UNICEF, 2019, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.BASW.ZS?locations=BS
- **Shelter, Not measured:** Neither safely managed sanitation nor urban slums is measured for the Bahamas. WHO/UNICEF, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.STA.SMSS.ZS?locations=BS
- **Energy, 100%:** 100% cook with clean fuels and technology; 100% have electricity. World Bank, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.CFT.ACCS.ZS?locations=BS
- **Education, 95.3:** 95.3% of young people complete lower secondary school; 2010, dated. World Bank, 2010, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.SEC.CMPT.LO.ZS?locations=BS
- **Health, 80:** WHO service coverage index. WHO, 2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~BHS
- **Livelihoods, Not measured:** No poverty survey for the Bahamas is published by the World Bank. World Bank, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.UMIC?locations=BS

Furthest from reach: Food (17.2%). Of Bahamians are moderately or severely food insecure.

- **$124M:** Estimated savings for marine conservation over the next 15 years. IDB, 2024, https://www.iadb.org/en/news/bahamas-launches-debt-ocean-conservation-swap-support-idb
