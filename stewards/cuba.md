## The seven standards

Re-measured 12 November 2026 on the delivery test: where Cuba’s own agencies publish how much of a service actually reaches people, the standard takes the lower of the strict measure and that delivered share. The first edition (10 November 2026) read only World Bank indicators of connection and access (electricity access 100%, clean cooking 96%, basic water 95.2%, WHO service coverage index 86), which counted Cubans as served while the grid meets about a third of demand. Computed in `tools/measures/cuba_2026_11_12.py`.

Weekly review of 23 November 2026: the figures are unchanged. No Unión Eléctrica note newer than 10 November 2026 opened on Granma or Cubadebate; a secondary report of the note of 15 November 2026 (Directorio Cubano, https://www.directoriocubano.info/?p=159793) gives 970 MW expected at the peak against 2,800 MW of demand (970 / 2,800 = 34.6%, arithmetic), which would not change the weakest standard and is not used. No newer INRH figure was found. The official Spanish text of the President’s speech of 8 August 2026 was not found on the Presidencia or Cubadebate sites. The World Bank API did not open.

- **Food, Not measured:** No FAO food insecurity survey (FIES) is published for Cuba. WFP: the state ration (canasta normada) covered only 46% of the recommended energy intake in 2023 (2,100 kcal reference). WFP, Cuba country strategic plan 2026 to 2030, WFP/EB.A/2026/8-A/2, 27 May 2026, https://executiveboard.wfp.org/document_download/WFP-0000173486, accessed 12 November 2026 and re-opened 23 November 2026: the regulated food basket “in 2023 already covered only 46 percent of the recommended energy intake.”
- **Water, 71.6%:** 2,681,770 people had their piped supply cut at dawn on 3 November 2026 (Antonio Rodriguez, President of INRH, Criterio Compartido, Radio Rebelde, 4 November 2026; Cubadebate, https://www.cubadebate.cu/noticias/2026/11/04/diaz-canel-sobre-el-abasto-de-agua-en-criterio-compartido-responder-al-pueblo-con-hechos-y-no-con-retorica/). 1 - 2,681,770 / 9,434,593 = 71.6%. Basic water 95.2% (WHO/UNICEF JMP, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.BASW.ZS?locations=CU). 66% of the cuts were caused by power cuts at pumping stations (INRH, same programme).
- **Shelter, 45.9%:** Safely managed sanitation; 11% of city dwellers live in slums. WHO/UNICEF, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.STA.SMSS.ZS?locations=CU
- **Energy, 33.4%:** The Unión Eléctrica (UNE) daily note of 10 November 2026: 970 MW available against 2,900 MW of peak demand, a deficit of 1,950 MW and 1,960 MW of load cut at the peak; on 9 November supply was cut for want of generation for all 24 hours. Granma, https://www.granma.cu/cuba/2026-11-10/afectacion-10-11-2026-07-11-12. 970 / 2,900 = 33.4%. Clean cooking 96% (World Bank, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.CFT.ACCS.ZS?locations=CU) and electricity access 100% measure connection, not supply.
- **Education, 89.2:** 89.2% of young people complete lower secondary school. World Bank, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.SEC.CMPT.LO.ZS?locations=CU
- **Health, 30%:** “Only 30% of the basic list of medicines is available, even though we are capable of producing all of our medicines.” (President of Cuba, closing speech of the XXIV International Meeting of Communist and Workers’ Parties, Havana, 8 August 2026; text published 10 August 2026, https://communistparty.ie/en/2026/08/it-is-not-enough-to-condemn-the-empire-we-must-build-popular-power-at-the-grassroots-level-win-the-streets/, re-opened 23 November 2026). The same 30% was given by the Minister of Public Health to the National Assembly health commission in July 2025 (30% in health units, 32% in pharmacies). WHO service coverage index 86 (2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~CUB).
- **Livelihoods, Not measured:** No poverty survey is published for Cuba. WFP: “a Cuban household needs ten to eleven times the minimum monthly salary” to buy a food basket covering its members’ caloric needs (2023 estimate). WFP, same document.

Furthest from reach: Health (70%). Of the basic list of medicines is not available to Cubans.

## The register (10 lines)

Each line links its own source. Opened on 23 November 2026.

- **EU humanitarian aid for the Cuban population, €10 million (new):** European Commission, DG ECHO, 23 July 2026 (https://civil-protection-humanitarian-aid.ec.europa.eu/news-stories/news/eu-releases-additional-eur10-million-humanitarian-aid-cuban-population-2026-07-23_en): “an additional €10 million in humanitarian aid”; for “procuring medicines and supporting primary and maternal healthcare facilities”, “emergency food assistance and nutrition treatment for children and mothers”, “rehabilitation of water systems and supplies to treat water”, “logistics support to hard-to-reach areas and renewable energy solutions”; “EU humanitarian aid is solely channelled through humanitarian partners working on the ground.” It adds to €4 million approved in early 2026 as a Caribbean regional allocation, mainly for Cuba, and €2 million in April 2026; nearly €6 million was mobilised for Cuba in 2025.
- **Canada, new assistance to help Cubans, C$5.5 million (new):** Global Affairs Canada, “April 17, 2026 - Ottawa, Ontario” (https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/04/canada-provides-new-assistance-to-help-cubans.html): “an additional $5.5 million in international assistance”; “The Pan American Health Organization will receive $5 million from this new funding”, to “improve availability of and access to essential health services, critical medicines and medical supplies”; the World Food Programme receives the rest to “provide food assistance to Cubans and support essential logistics and fuel needs for the humanitarian response.” Amounts in Canadian dollars.
- **EU humanitarian aid, logistics for relief, €2 million (new):** European Commission, DG ECHO, 1 April 2026 (https://civil-protection-humanitarian-aid.ec.europa.eu/news-stories/news/eu-releases-additional-eu2-million-humanitarian-aid-cuban-population-2026-04-01_en): an additional €2 million for logistics support to partners delivering “urgent relief to the most vulnerable”, amid the energy crisis and disruptions to the distribution of food and drinking water.
- **Canada, food and nutrition for vulnerable Cubans, C$8 million (new):** Global Affairs Canada, “February 25, 2026 - Ottawa, Ontario” (https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/02/canada-provides-assistance-to-the-people-of-cuba.html): “an accelerated funding of $8 million to be released immediately” “to scale-up food and nutrition for vulnerable Cubans”, “delivered through trusted partnerships with the World Food Programme and the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF)”; “Canada provided over $3 million in humanitarian assistance” after Hurricane Melissa (October 2025). Amounts in Canadian dollars.
- **United States, humanitarian supplies through the Catholic Church, $6 million (new; news only):** Associated Press (Dánica Coto), 5 February 2026, as published by KPRC Click2Houston (https://www.click2houston.com/news/world/2026/02/05/us-announces-6m-in-aid-for-cuba-as-islands-leader-accuses-it-of-imposing-an-energy-blockade/): “an additional $6 million in aid for Cuba”, “said U.S. Department of State Senior Official Jeremy Lewin”; “The supplies include rice, beans, pasta, cans of tuna and solar lamps”; “that will be delivered by the Catholic Church and Caritas”; “The U.S. previously sent $3 million in disaster relief to Cuban people affected by Melissa.” No State Department page was found.
- **WFP Cuba country strategic plan 2026–2030, $116.4 million (new):** see the record.
- **Mi Costa, GCF FP157, $23.9 million (new; approved before 2024, still active):** Green Climate Fund, project page (https://www.greenclimate.fund/project/FP157): “Coastal Resilience to Climate Change in Cuba through Ecosystem Based Adaptation - “MI COSTA””; accredited entity “United Nations Development Programme”; GCF grant USD 23,927,294, 83% disbursed; co-financing USD 20,371,935; total USD 44.3 million; approved 19 March 2021 at B.28; financial agreement effective 13 September 2021, estimated completion 13 September 2029; “Under implementation”; “over 1.3 million vulnerable people living in the target coastal communities”.
- **IRES-Cuba, GCF FP126, $38.2 million (new; approved before 2024, still active):** FAO, “12 March 2020, Havana/Rome” (https://www.fao.org/americas/news/news-detail/Green-Climate-Fund-approves-a-119-million-climate-resilient-project-for-Cuba/en): “The Green Climate Fund will contribute $38.2 million”; “the Government of Cuba provides $81.7 million in co-financing”; “The seven-year project”; “expected to help 240 000 people”, including “52 000 family farmers”; “agroforestry practices on 35 000 hectares”; to “improve water infiltration and retention in soils”.
