## Sources of the figures that carry no link of their own

### The hero
- **$3B, Eurobonds restructured, 2024; the lead, restructured $3 billion of bonds in 2024:** The Republic of Zambia, represented by the Ministry of Finance, Final Results Announcement, 4 June 2024 (https://dev.emta.org/media/1ozdf0kx/republic_of_zambia_results_announcement.pdf, opened 11 November 2026): the notes restructured were “U.S.$750,000,000 5.375 per cent. Notes due 2022”, “U.S.$1,000,000,000 8.500 per cent. Notes due 2024” and “U.S.$1,250,000,000 8.970 per cent. Notes due 2027”; $3 billion is their sum (arithmetic). The proposal was approved by holders of 97.23%, 94.06% and 97.42% of the outstanding principal of the three series; settlement was expected on 11 June 2024. The IMF staff report for the third review (https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1zmbea2024001-print-pdf.pdf.md) records the exchange as implemented on 11 June 2024, for new bonds of $1.72 billion due 2033 and $1.36 billion due 2053. It replaces Daily Maverick (4 June 2024), a news source.
- **35.3%, natural resource rents, share of GDP:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2021; not re-read.
- **41.0%, undernourished, 2024; the lead, four in ten citizens are undernourished:** FAO prevalence of undernourishment, via World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2024; not re-read.
- **The lead, a nation of 21.9 million:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, population 2025 (21,913,874, the `population` of the nation file); not re-read.

### The register and the record (each line links its own source)
- **2053 Eurobond buyback:** Republic of Zambia, tender offer announcement of 4 June 2026 (as published on Stockopedia): “Fixed Rate Step-Up Amortising Notes due 2053”, outstanding principal U.S.$1,364,725,564; Tender Consideration of U.S.$740 per U.S.$1,000 plus accrued interest, with a U.S.$40 early fee and a share of a U.S.$65,000,000 pooled fee; no tender accepted below 75% of the notes; described as a “debt for development swap” for the energy sector, with US$275 million to be invested over 15 years. African Development Bank release of 24 June 2026 (via allAfrica): the Government “used a $600 million loan from the African Development Bank Group” and its own resources “to buy back the $1.36 billion sovereign Eurobond”, and “committed to earmark $275 million, to be invested in the country’s energy sector”, and calls it “the successful $1.36 billion debt buyback”. The IMF staff report for the third review (above) gives the 2053 bond as one of the two bonds issued in the 2024 exchange.
