## The register (3 lines)

Sources opened on 19 November 2026 unless stated.

- **OPEC Fund, El Tortuguero–La Esperanza Rural Electricity Transmission Project ($20.5M, active):** OPEC Fund US$20.50 million of a total project cost of US$23.00 million; approval date “15.04.2020”, signing date “21.07.2020”, status “Ongoing”, Energy, Public Sector; the project aims “to enhance energy access in remote communities” in the southern Caribbean coastal region; “Works will include expanding infrastructure and grid connections for circa 300,000 people.”, https://opecfund.org/operations/list/el-tortuguero-la-esperanza-rural-electricity-transmission-project
- **CABEI, Programme for the Improvement and Expansion of Four Drinking Water Systems in Five Cities ($130M, signed):** CABEI, Tegucigalpa, 25 September 2024: “approved a new financing of US$130 million for the Republic of Nicaragua”; Ocotal, Camoapa, Diriamba, San Jorge and Buenos Aires; 26 new water supply sources, more than 11,700 household connections installed or rehabilitated and more than 27 million cubic metres of drinking water a year; “expected to benefit more than 140,000 Nicaraguans by 2026”; “The general objective of this program is to improve the standard of living and quality of life”, https://www.bcie.org/en/news-and-media/news/article/bcie-aprueba-nuevo-financiamiento-dirigido-al-programa-de-mejoramiento-y-ampliacion-en-4-sistemas-de-agua-potable-en-5-ciudades-de-nicaragua . ## The enterprises

Opened 19 November 2026.

- **CacaoNica (Livelihood):** Quixote Center, 22 January 2026: a cooperative in Waslala, “Nicaragua’s Northern Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region”; “Founded 25 years ago by 69 smallholder farmers, the cooperative was born out of a shared commitment” to improving life for cocoa-growing families; 372 members; “The cooperative now exports Rainforest Alliance–certified cocoa to Europe, allowing members to earn $1.20 per pound”; an energy and sanitation pilot with Green Empowerment and PeaceWorks from August 2025, after which “By December 2025, 40 families had experienced meaningful improvements in their daily lives”, https://quixote.org/posts/lighting-the-path-forward-inspiring-results-from-our-nicaragua-pilot-with-cacaonica
- **SOPPEXCCA (Livelihood):** Sustainable Harvest, “Posted by Ana Valencia on March 3, 2026”: a Union of Agricultural Cooperatives (UCA SOPPEXCCA); “We only sell coffee from our members, who now number 405 producers.”; “Young women can get small loans to start businesses like shops, raising animals, or poultry farming.”; “Today, 42 of our 135 women members hold leadership positions within their cooperatives.”; a food security programme and a breast cancer prevention campaign this year, https://www.sustainableharvest.com/blog/women-quality-and-long-term-relationships-the-journey-of-soppexcca-with-fatima-ismael . Root Capital (undated): a Fair Trade and organic coffee cooperative in Jinotega selling “Las Hermanas coffee” from its women producers, https://rootcapital.org/meet-our-clients/stories/soppexcca-empowering-women-coffee-farmers-in-nicaragua/
- **Fundenuse (Livelihood):** Oikocredit, data “Last synced with latest available data on: January 10, 2025”, document of 6 February 2025: “Fundación para el Desarrollo de Nueva Segovia (FUNDENUSE)”, which “offers loans and technical assistance to (female) micro entrepreneurs and farmers” in northern and central Nicaragua; Oikocredit partner since 2004; financing for “Fresh working capital to support portfolio expansion”; 22,906 clients, 55.2% women, 65.6% rural, https://my.oikocredit.coop/l/en/oikoproject/project/10539/pdf . Kiva also lists loans to Nicaraguan borrowers through Fundenuse (https://kiva.org/lend-classic/2877511).
- **PRODESA (Livelihood):** Oikocredit, data of 10 January 2025, document of 7 February 2025: “Corporación para la Promoción y Desarrollo Nicaragua (PRODESA)”, Juigalpa; microloans and credit lines to smallholder and mid-sized farmers and entrepreneurs in six regions of the remote Atlantic area, where “Muchas personas en esta parte del país no tienen acceso a servicios financieros básicos”; PRODESA “utilizará el apoyo de Oikocredit para seguir ampliando su cartera y consolidar sus sucursales”; 21,433 active borrowers, “Clientas 49.1%”, rural clients 37.8%, https://my.oikocredit.coop/l/es/oikoproject/project/31496/pdf
- **MiCrédito (Livelihood):** Oikocredit, data of 10 January 2025, document of 6 February 2025: “MiCrédito will use Oikocredit’s loan to extend loans to new clients”; it “aims to become market leader for financial products in rural areas”; 11,988 clients, 59.3% women, 39.6% rural, https://my.oikocredit.coop/l/en/oikoproject/project/25919/pdf
