The IMF gives a different series: “Stock of public debt: 43.4 percent of GDP (2024), 40.3 (2025), 39.6 (2026)” (IMF Press Release 26/096, 30 March 2026, https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/03/30/pr-26096-cameroon-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation).
- **65.8%, electricity from renewable sources, 2021:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, renewable electricity output (% of total), 2021; not re-read.
- **The lead, a nation of 29.9 million:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, population 2025 (29,879,337, the `population` of the nation file); forest 40.7% of land (2024) and renewable electricity 65.8% (2021), as in the Sovereign Assets; not re-read.
- **The lead, the IMF programmes completed in 2025:** IMF Press Release 25/265, 24 July 2025 (https://www.imf.org/en/News/Articles/2025/07/24/pr-25265-cameroon-imf-concludes-8th-rev-under-ecf-and-eff-and-3rd-rev-under-rsf; see the register): the eighth and final reviews of the Extended Credit Facility and the Extended Fund Facility and the third and final review of the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. ## The register (13 lines)

Each line links its own source. Opened on 23 November 2026 unless stated.

- **International bond by private placement, $750M (Issued):** see the record.
- **World Bank, Douala–Bangui Economic Corridor Program, Phase 1, $425M for Cameroon:** World Bank, “World Bank Approves US$1.12 Billion Program to Modernize the Douala–Bangui Economic Corridor”, Washington, 12 June 2026: “The World Bank Group Board of Executive Directors today approved a US$1.12 billion Multiphase Programmatic Approach” program; Phase 1 of US$525 million, with “US$407 million from IBRD and US$18 million from IDA to Cameroon”, “US$90 million from IDA to the CAR” and “US$10 million from the IDA GROW window to CEMAC” ($407 million + $18 million = $425 million, arithmetic); barriers that “choke trade, raise consumer prices, undermine food security”; “about 2,000 to 4,000 direct and indirect job opportunities over the project lifecycle”.
- **World Bank, Sustainable Cities and Land Project, $200M:** World Bank, “New Project Boosts Infrastructure and Land Services in Cameroon”, 30 May 2025: climate-resilient urban infrastructure in Yaoundé and Douala and better land administration; to “reach an estimated 2.1 million people in Cameroon”. The release states no Board date.
- **World Bank, Cameroon Water Security Program, Phase 1, $200M:** World Bank, “World Bank Approves First Phase in a Holistic Approach to Addressing Water Security in Cameroon”, 7 May 2025: the World Bank “has today approved the first phase of the Multiphase Programmatic Approach”; three phases over 11 years ($200 million, $400 million and $350 million; $950 million in all); “expected to provide drinking water to about 3.9 million people and sanitation to 2.9 million people”.
- **IFC, senior loan to Afriland First Bank, up to $60M:** IFC, “IFC and Afriland First Bank Cameroon Partner to Boost SME Financing in Cameroon”, Yaoundé, 2 April 2025: a senior loan of “up to $60 million equivalent in euros”, $20 million from IFC’s own account and $40 million mobilized from other lenders, for lending to small and medium enterprises with a focus on women entrepreneurs; the Women Entrepreneurs Opportunity Facility offers “performance-based incentives up to 0.07% of the outstanding IFC senior loan”. The release gives no signing date.
- **World Bank, Fiscal Sustainability DPF and IMPACT Program-for-Results, $400M:** World Bank, “World Bank Group Supports Cameroon’s Economic Resilience, Domestic Revenue Mobilization, and Transparency of Public Finances”, 17 March 2025: two operations of $400 million in all, approved by the Board of Executive Directors on 14 March 2025, IBRD; the DPF is the second of three operations in a 2022–2026 series; IMPACT is to enable improvements in basic services such as “healthcare and water supply”.
- **IMF, Resilience and Sustainability Facility (Completed):** IMF Press Release 25/265, 24 July 2025: RSF approved in January 2024 with “SDR 138 million (US$ 181.7 million, or 50 percent of quota)”; this review made “SDR 51.75 million (US$71 million)” available; total RSF disbursements “SDR 120.75 million (US$162.3 million)”. IMF Country Report 25/222, Executive Summary (https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1cmrea2025002-source-pdf.pdf.md): “The eighth and final ECF-EFF reviews and the third and final RSF review”; “Financing for the uncompleted RSF reform will not become available upon program expiration.” The report gives the RSF approval both as 29 January 2024 and, in a table, as 22 January 2024.
- **IMF, Extended Credit Facility and Extended Fund Facility (Completed):** IMF Press Release 25/265, 24 July 2025: this review made “SDR 55.2 million (about US$75.7 million)” available; total disbursements “SDR 593.4 million (US$793.8 million)”; the arrangements were extended in December 2023 by “12 months”; IMF Country Report 25/222: “eighth and final ECF-EFF reviews”. IMF Press Release 26/314, 1 October 2026 (https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/10/01/pr26314-imf-staff-concludes-assesment-cameroon): staff “held discussions in Yaounde from September 17 to 30, 2026” under a post-financing assessment; the economy “grew 3.5 percent in 2025”; “Average inflation has continued to decline to 2.6 percent through August 2026”; “The debt sustainability analysis remains at high overall risk of debt distress.” No page opened states the expiry date of the arrangements.
- **IsDB, Douala–Bafoussam Road Rehabilitation Project, €212.35M (Signed):** Islamic Development Bank, “IsDB and Cameroon Sign EUR 212.35 Million Financing Agreement for Major Transport Project”: signed in Bangkok on 13 October 2026, on the margins of the World Bank Group and IMF Annual Meetings, by Dr. Muhammad Al Jasser, Chairman of the IsDB Group, and Alamine Ousmane Mey, Minister of Economy, Planning and Regional Development; 218 km of national roads and 108 km of feeder roads; “classrooms, health centres, water facilities, markets, and agricultural facilities”. Camer.be, 14 October 2026 (news): about FCFA 139.2 billion; total project cost €229.93 million; 20 years with a five-year grace period.
- **African Development Bank, Agro-Industry in the North, Phase 1 (PDAS-1), €81.2M:** African Development Bank release of 14 July 2026, as carried by allAfrica (https://allafrica.com/stories/202607150647.html; the afdb.org page was not opened): “The Board of Directors of the African Development Bank Group” approved “an €81.2 million loan” for the “Programme for the Development of Agro-Industry in the North: Construction of Multi-purpose Hillside Dams (PDAS-1)”, “a total cost of €88.74 million”, in the North and Far North. Investir au Cameroun (news) gives the approval date as 13 July 2026, with “three multi-purpose hillside dams and the development of 618 hectares of irrigated farmland”.
- **African Development Bank, Opening up the East, Phase 1 (PDCBET), €309.93M:** African Development Bank release of 9 March 2026, as carried by allAfrica (https://allafrica.com/stories/202603110622.html; the afdb.org page was not opened): approved by the Board of Directors on 18 February 2026; a loan of €309.93 million for the first phase of the “Programme for the Opening-up and Connectivity of Cross-Border Economic Basins in the Eastern Region (PDCBET)”: 156 kilometres of the Ngoura II to Yokadouma road; at least 2,500 direct and indirect jobs expected.
- **FMO, senior loan to AFG Bank Cameroon, €30M (Approved):** FMO project detail, published 1 April 2026: “FMO has approved a EUR 30mln senior loan to AFG CM”, part of a syndication “for a total amount of up to EUR 65 million”, “to provide AFG CM with long-term funding to support the expansion of lending to the private sector”; status “Investment in contracting phase”; an EFSD+ guarantee with “a particular focus on businesses run by women, young people and migrants”.
- **Proparco, ARIZ portfolio guarantee for Advans Cameroun, FCFA 1.3bn:** Proparco project page: signed “January 26, 2024”; “XAF 1,311,914,000 guarantee”, EUR 2,000,000; a “risk sub-participation of FCFA 1.3bn (EUR 2m)”; smallholder farmers in rural areas in the maize and broiler sectors; “Access to financial inclusion for 564 beneficiaries”. Proparco news, 29 January 2024: “risk sub-participation covering 80% of a total loan portfolio of 1.6 billion FCFA”; five Proparco guarantees to Advans Cameroun since 2010.

## The enterprises

- **KingsCombo Sarl (Food):** FAO, Empowerment of Young Agricultural Producers in Africa (EYPA) Mentorship Programme, Finalists 2025 (18 November 2025, Kigali), https://www.fao.org/partnerships/documents/document/es/c/1755426 : Nora Kungang Ambotaba, Cameroon, KingsCombo Sarl: “KingsCombo specializes in maize cultivation and value chain enhancement”, with maize derivatives, farmer training and consultancy. Evidence of 2023; no newer page opened.
- **Mpokem Gaz (Energy):** Le360 Afrique, “Cameroun : le foyer écologique qui transforme la pénurie de gaz en opportunité”, 3 August 2026, https://afrique.le360.ma/societe/cameroun-le-foyer-ecologique-qui-transforme-la-penurie-de-gaz-en-opportunite_3RJVHNOBCFC6BP2X32AWFAY22U/ : a young start-up based in Douala; a stove made mainly of salvaged sheet metal and cement, “Le ciment qui y est introduit sert à concentrer la chaleur pour une cuisson rapide des aliments”, with a fan run from a power bank recharged by a solar panel; 28,000 FCFA for a single burner and 100,000 FCFA for the multifunctional model; a buyer: “Je suis venue spécialement ici pour m’acheter ce foyer.”
- **Waspito (Health):** Africa’s Business Heroes, 2025 Top 10, Jean Lobe Lobe, https://africabusinessheroes.org/en/the-prize/2025/top10/jean-lobe-lobe : headquartered in Douala; “a social healthcare network designed to be a one-stop platform for all healthcare needs”; 850,000 users and 100,000 paying clients (the page’s statistics also show 1M+ users and 2,500 care providers). MIT Solve, “Waspito” (https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/73197): “Telehealth platform which provides instant video consultations, mobile laboratory and pharmacy services”; operating in Cameroon and Côte d’Ivoire; it earns “a commission from the providers fee, ranging from 10% -30% on some services”.
- **Efarm.cm (Livelihood):** World Bank, “Connected Farmers, Better Harvests: How Cameroon’s Agritech Innovators Are Transforming Agriculture”, 25 June 2026, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/06/25/connected-farmers-better-harvests-how-cameroon-agritech-innovators-are-transforming-agriculture : its Director General, Bang Pamela Ncho, says the platform assists farmers with marketing as well as listing their products on its website. The feature gives no reach figures and no revenue model.
- **Advans Cameroun (Livelihood):** Proparco, “Advans Cameroun assists SMEs and small farms with the support of Proparco”, 29 January 2024, https://proparco.fr/en/actualites/advans-cameroun-assists-smes-and-small-farms-support-proparco : a licensed microfinance institution that began operating in 2007 and provides “financial services that meet the needs of small and medium-sized businesses”, craftspeople, traders and entrepreneurs; the guarantee supports its credit in rural areas, especially agriculture. Advans Cameroun is a member of the Advans group.
