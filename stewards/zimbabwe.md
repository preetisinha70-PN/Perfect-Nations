## Sources of the figures that carry no link of their own

### The hero
- **$782M, lithium exports, first half of 2026; the lead, lithium exports more than tripled in a year, to $782 million in the first half of 2026:** Minister of Finance, Economic Development and Investment Promotion, 2026 Mid-Term Budget and Economic Review, presented to Parliament on 30 July 2026 (Hansard, https://e-portal.parlzim.gov.zw/files/HAN-1785429915691.pdf, opened 11 November 2026), para. 19: “export earnings of lithium products grew substantially by 229.8% to US$782.2 million during the first half of 2026”, from US$237.2 million in the first half of 2025 (3.3 times, arithmetic). It replaces MINING.COM (Reuters), 30 July 2026, a news source reporting the same statement.
- **50%, of electricity from renewables, 2022:** IRENA, Zimbabwe statistical profile, last updated 31 July 2024 (https://www.irena.org/IRENADocuments/Statistical_Profiles/Africa/Zimbabwe_Africa_RE_SP.pdf, opened 11 November 2026): renewable generation 6,163 GWh of 12,282 GWh in 2022 (50.2%, arithmetic; IRENA shows 50%); non-renewable 6,118 GWh; renewables 75% of total energy supply in 2021. It replaces “87.5%, renewable share of electricity output, 2021” (World Bank, World Development Indicators). The later IRENA profile (2023 data) could not be opened in this review; the address above returned the edition of 31 July 2024.
- **62.0%, with electricity, 2024; the lead, a nation of 17.0 million:** World Bank, World Development Indicators, access to electricity (EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS), 2024, and population 2025 (16,950,795, the `population` of the nation file); not re-read.
- **The lead, 38% lived in extreme poverty in 2019:** ZIMSTAT and World Bank, Zimbabwe Poverty Update 2017–19 (https://www.zimstat.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/Macro/Poverty_Statistics/Zimbabwe_PovertyUpdate_2017-2019_WEBB_Final.pdf, opened 11 November 2026): “Extreme poverty rose from 30 percent in 2017 to 38 percent in April–May 2019” (below the Food Poverty Line of US$29.80 per person per month; 38.3%, Figure 4.1). Total consumption poverty was 70.4% in 2017 (Table 4.1). The former wording, “four in ten citizens are poor”, used a broader term than the source’s “extreme poverty”.
