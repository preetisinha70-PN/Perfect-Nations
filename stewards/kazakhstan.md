## The seven standards

Not re-read at source on 19 November 2026.

- **Food, 98%:** Not food insecure; 2% are moderately or severely food insecure. FAO, 2023, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?locations=KZ
- **Water, 91.6%:** Safely managed drinking water. WHO/UNICEF, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?locations=KZ
- **Shelter, 99.2%:** City dwellers outside slums; 0.8% live in slums; safely managed sanitation is not measured. UN-Habitat, 2022, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EN.POP.SLUM.UR.ZS?locations=KZ
- **Energy, 96%:** 96% cook with clean fuels and technology; 100% have electricity. World Bank, 2024, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.CFT.ACCS.ZS?locations=KZ
- **Education, 89.6:** 89.6% of children read a simple text by the end of primary school. World Bank, 2021, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SE.LPV.PRIM?locations=KZ
- **Health, 83:** WHO service coverage index. WHO, 2023, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/universal-health-coverage-index?country=~KAZ
- **Livelihoods, 84.8%:** Above US$8.30 a day; 15.2% live below it. World Bank, 2021, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.UMIC?locations=KZ

Furthest from reach: Livelihood (15.2%). Of Kazakhstanis live below US$8.30 a day.

## The enterprises

Opened 19 November 2026.

- **Global Kishkentai (Education):** “started creating toys for preschool children with disabilities”; wooden educational sets; “average monthly turnover of ₸12 million ($26,579)”; with the start-up CodiPlay on programming kits “for schoolchildren in Kazakhstan, South Korea, and recently in Saudi Arabia”. QazMonitor, 10 April 2024, https://en.qazmonitor.com/digital/2839/who-drives-social-entrepreneurship-in-kazakhstan . The same article reports the national register of social enterprises: “as of April 10, 2024, the list has grown to include 436 registered SEs”.
- **AR Sana (Health):** a mobile app using artificial intelligence and augmented reality to help Kazakh-speaking children develop their speech, from about 18 months, with the AR Sana Lab platform for specialists; more than 35 specialists in 5 centres and 2 schools; pilots in Astana, Semey and Almaty; full access for parents at $9.99 a month and an Enterprise plan at $1,500 a year; “A speech therapy center paid 89,000 tenge before the product even launched.” Digital Business, 29 May 2026, https://digitalbusiness.kz/en/2026-05-29/a-kazakh-founder-invested-7-million-tenge-to-launch-a-startup-helping-kids-develop-speech-skills/
- **KMF Bank (Livelihood):** “one of the country’s leading microfinance institutions”, 18 branches and 117 sales points; “Over 55 percent of its borrowers are women”; “67 percent live in rural areas”; IFC supports its transformation into “a bank with a strong MSE focus”. IFC, 10 September 2024, https://ifc.org/en/pressroom/2024/ifc-helps-boost-financing-for-micro-and-smaller-businesses-in-kazakhstan . Operating as KMF Bank in 2026 (EBRD, https://www.ebrd.com/home/news-and-events/news/2026/ebrd-provides-us--50-million-loan-to-kmf-bank.html). Earned income: interest on its loans.
- **Arnur Credit (Livelihood):** a microfinance institution, eighth by assets, that “serves more than 25,000 clients in rural areas of Southern Kazakhstan”; about half of its loan portfolio is “dedicated to women-led businesses”. IFC, 14 July 2025, https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2025/ifc-partners-with-arnur-credit-to-expand-access-to-finance-for-women-entrepreneurs . Earned income: interest on its loans.
- **Kunde café (Livelihood):** “Kunde café operates in Astana and Almaty with about 50 staff, including around 35 employees with disabilities”; “including 25 people with special needs in Astana and 12 in Almaty”; “a small coffee island in the Atakent Mall designed specifically for wheelchair users”; “the café began as a volunteer project during his first year of university”; opened with crowdfunding on StartTime.kz. The Astana Times, 6 December 2025, https://astanatimes.com/?p=109685 . Also named as a café that “employs vulnerable individuals” (QazMonitor, 2024). Earned income: café sales.
- **GreenTal (Livelihood):** “GreenTal company, which is a carpentry workshop, a sewing workshop, and a printing shop that employs people from socially vulnerable groups, which also include single mothers or reformed ex-convicts”; its co-founders Asset Akhmadiyev and Emin Askerov “co-created a special space that combines a coffee shop, a showroom and a Social Innovation Hub” “to showcase products from 11 inclusive workshops”. Euronews, 18 February 2026, https://www.euronews.com/2026/02/18/from-classrooms-to-workplaces-kazakhstans-evolving-journey-toward-equal-opportunities . Earned income: the sale of its workshops’ products and services.
