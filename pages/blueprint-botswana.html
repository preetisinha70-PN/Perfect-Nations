Sovereign Assets

Five assets no balance sheet counts.

Each is owned today, earns today, and can lift Botswana closer to Perfect Capita.

  • 01

    The Diamonds

    80%Of exports, IMF, 2025

    About a quarter of GDP. The 2025 agreement raises the state’s share of Debswana’s sales to 30%, then 40%.

  • 02

    The Sun

    0.2%Renewable share of electricity output, 2021

    A grid run almost wholly on coal, under a desert sun. The largest gap between asset and use on this page.

  • 03

    The Delta

    27.8%Of land under forest, 2024

    The Okavango and its woodlands: a wildlife economy run on high-value, low-volume terms.

  • 04

    The Land

    45.6%Of land in agriculture, 2024

    Rangeland and cattle across nearly half the country, while 18.2% of people are undernourished.

  • 05

    The People

    2.56mCitizens, 2025

    Two in three live in towns and cities.

What each asset is worth, and how far it can carry Botswana toward Perfect Capita, is the question our research and our guests take up below.

Design Studio · Perfect Capita

The Botswana wheel: held at 36.8 by livelihood

  • To setPerfect CapitaThe yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards, set with Botswana in its own prices.
  • To setThe price of Perfect BotswanaPerfect Capita times the population: the national bill, each year.
  • 36.8CoverageOut of 100, set by livelihood, the standard Botswana provides least.
  • ~1.6mThe GapBatswana living below US$8.30 a day, on the 2015 survey.

The Living LineThe poverty line measures income. The Living Line measures a life: food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and livelihood. Perfect Capita is the yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards. Coverage scores each standard strictly out of 100, and a nation is held at its weakest: Botswana at 36.8, by livelihood, with food at 48 and education at 49.2 next.

Perfect Nations estimate, to be confirmed with Botswana. Figures marked dated rest on surveys more than five years old; lenient measures are marked as such. The strict measures

Strict measures. Food: share not moderately or severely food insecure (FAO). Water: safely managed drinking water where measured; where it is not, basic water, marked as such. Shelter: the lower of safely managed sanitation and the share outside urban slums. Energy: the lower of electricity per person against 1,000 kWh and clean cooking. Education: the lower of children reading by the end of primary school and lower-secondary completion. Health: WHO service coverage index. Livelihood: share above the stricter of the national poverty line and the World Bank line for the nation’s income group. Each figure links to its source.
