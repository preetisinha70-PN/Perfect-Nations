Dear Ghana,

Red, gold and green, with the Black Star of African freedom at the centre. You were the first nation south of the Sahara to win its independence, and you have just completed one of the hardest economic recoveries on the continent.

Think of what you have already built. In July 2026 the IMF completed the last review of your $3 billion programme, and your economy grew 6 percent in 2025. In 2026 the World Bank approved $500 million for rural roads and markets and $300 million for secondary schools and skills.

At home the work has begun. Eighty-five in every hundred of your young people complete lower-secondary school. Power has passed peacefully between parties at the ballot box again and again, and your cocoa and gold earn hard currency every season.

The work ahead is plain, and it is worth saying plainly. Only nineteen in every hundred of you have a safely managed toilet at home. Only thirty-five in every hundred of you cook with clean fuel. Only forty-three in every hundred of you drink safely managed water at home. Shelter sets your Coverage today, at 18.8 out of 100, with clean energy and water close behind. Each of them is within your reach.

Now imagine that same resolve turned toward every home in the country. Picture Perfect Ghana: a morning when every family cooks with clean fuel and sits down to a full table; when every tap runs clean and every home has a safe toilet; when every child walks to a school where a teacher is waiting, and reads with ease by the age of ten; when every mother lives within reach of a clinic with the lights on, and every young person finds work that pays. And no Ghanaian falls below a decent life, because the nation has decided that none will.

None of this needs a miracle. It needs what you already own: your gold, your cocoa, your forest, the Volta and a recovery the world has watched you earn. They are assets no balance sheet counts, and together they can carry every Ghanaian to food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and a livelihood.

“You brought your economy back from the edge. Now bring a safe toilet to every Ghanaian home.”

So this is an invitation. Bring your vision, your story and your solutions to the Perfect Ghana Design Studio, and join the Co-designers who are already imagining what comes next. Perfect Ghana will not be designed for you. It will be designed by you.

Yours in design,

Preeti Sinha

President and CEO, Perfect Nations
