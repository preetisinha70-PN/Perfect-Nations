  1. Solution No. 11Tanzania6 Nov 2026

    Tanzania brings nine banks to its railway with one guarantee

    • Livelihood
    • Food
    The challenge
    Farms, mines and traders in western Tanzania sit far from the port of Dar es Salaam, and the long haul costs them their income.
    FfD
    An African Development Bank guarantee of up to US$800 million, shared with insurers, brought nine banks to a US$1.2 billion railway loan.
    SI
    SI screens the next corridors for the same blend, Uganda first, and tracks the freight, jobs and farm-gate prices each new line moves.

    $1.2bncommercial facility for the Tabora–Kigoma railway

  2. Solution No. 10Barbados4 Nov 2026

    Barbados turns cheaper debt into health care

    • Health
    The challenge
    Barbados carries costly debt while chronic disease and mental health strain its health system.
    FfD
    A 20-year IDB guarantee of US$175 million lowers the cost of existing debt; about US$160 million of the savings is bound to health care.
    SI
    SI follows every dollar of the savings into the named sites and services, and screens fellow Caribbean borrowers, Saint Lucia first, for the same swap.

    $175mIDB guarantee for Barbados, 20-year term

  3. Solution No. 09Zambia2 Nov 2026

    Zambia turns a bond discount into power lines

    • Energy
    • Livelihood
    The challenge
    Unreliable supply and grid losses hold back the homes, clinics, schools and mines that run on electricity.
    FfD
    A US$600 million African Development Bank Group loan, with the government’s own funds, bought back a US$1.36 billion Eurobond; US$275 million of the savings is earmarked for electricity over 15 years.
    SI
    SI follows the earmark connection by connection, and screens every bond trading below face value, Mozambique first, for the next buyback.

    $275mearmarked for Zambia’s energy sector over 15 years

  5. Solution No. 06Ethiopia19 Oct 2026

    Ethiopia trades a haircut for a seat at its next bond

    • All seven, through the budget
    The challenge
    A defaulted US$1 billion Eurobond keeps Ethiopia out of the market its budgets depend on.

  8. Solution No. 03Kenya12 Oct 2026

    Kenya has bought time. Now it should buy down the price.

    • Health
    • Water
    • Energy
    The challenge
    Kenya pays 9.5% on the US$1.5 billion bond it sold in February 2025, money that does not reach a health system scoring 57 out of 100 on the WHO coverage index.
    FfD
    A guaranteed refinancing that lowers the coupon itself, with health, water and electricity targets written into the terms.
    SI
    SI prices the saving a guarantee would bring, and tracks the targets each year so the interest saved reaches the standards they measure.

Food

Pay before the harvest fails.

FfD · Sovereign drought insurance

Parametric cover pays on measured rainfall, so food reaches families within weeks of a failed season, without waiting for an appeal.

ProvenMalawi, 2024 $11.2M paid out after the El Niño drought, for food aid to about 235,000 households African Development Bank, August 2024

SI

SI reads rainfall, harvests and food prices district by district, sets the trigger and prices the cover before the season begins.

Health

Let lower debt costs pay for care.

FfD · Debt-for-health guarantee

A development bank guarantee cuts the cost of existing debt, and the savings are bound to the health system before they can be spent elsewhere.

ProvenBarbados, 2026 $175M IDB guarantee, 20 years; about US$160 million of the savings for health care Inter-American Development Bank, project BA-U0003

SI

SI prices the essential services each citizen lacks, finds the costly debt to refinance, and follows every saving into clinics and care.

In no open blueprint is health the standard furthest from reach. Solution No. 10 shows how Barbados pays for it.
