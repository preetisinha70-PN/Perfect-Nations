      BarbadosSolution No. 449 Nov 2026

      The Blue Food Swap

      ~88,000Barbadians moderately or severely food insecure

      The next debt conversion is bound to local farms, fisheries and sea farming.

      • Food
      • Livelihood
      The challenge
      About 88,000 Barbadians, three in ten, are moderately or severely food insecure.
      What Perfect Nations advises
      Barbados should make its next debt conversion a blue food swap, refinancing costly debt with guaranteed debt and binding the savings to local farms, fisheries and sea farming, so the island grows more of what it eats. With super intelligence, food prices, harvests and fish stocks can be read week by week, so support reaches the households that cannot count on enough good food before prices rise.

      Source: FAORead the Solution

      KenyaSolution No. 439 Nov 2026

      The Rift Reading Bond

      4 in 5Kenyan children who reach the end of primary school unable to read

      The challenge
      Four in five Kenyan children reach the end of primary school unable to read a simple text.
      What Perfect Nations advises
      Kenya should sell a reading bond to its own citizens by phone, as it did with M-Akiba, back it with part of the revenue from its geothermal power, and release funds to schools as children learn to read. With super intelligence, reading can be assessed in English and Kiswahili on a phone in every school, and every bondholder can see how many children their savings helped to read.

      Source: World BankRead the Solution

      IndiaSolution No. 429 Nov 2026

      Solar Schools, Reading Children

      56%of Indian children could not read a simple text, 2017

      The challenge
      56% of Indian children could not read a simple text by the end of primary school in 2017.
      What Perfect Nations advises
      India should put solar on the roof of every government school through its sovereign green bonds, and commit the electricity savings to reading programmes rewarded on results, as the Educate Girls impact bond first did in Rajasthan. With super intelligence, every child can have a reading tutor in their own language on a shared device, and every school’s progress and power savings can be seen side by side.

      Source: World BankRead the Solution

      BrazilSolution No. 419 Nov 2026

      Every Street Connected

      ~109mBrazilians without a safely managed toilet at home

      The challenge
      About 109 million Brazilians, about one in two, have no safely managed toilet at home.
      What Perfect Nations advises
      Brazil should issue a sanitation outcome bond under its sustainable bond framework, with the return rising as each verified household is connected to a safe toilet and sewer, and pair it with the currency hedging that Eco Invest Brasil already offers foreign investors. With super intelligence, every street without sewerage can be mapped from satellite and census data, so concessions are priced street by street and no community is left until last.

      Source: WHO/UNICEFRead the Solution

    5. 40

      RwandaSolution No. 409 Nov 2026

      Standard Tokens for Clean Kitchens

      ~13mRwandans who cook without clean fuel

      The challenge
      About 13 million Rwandans, nine in ten, cook without clean fuel or technology.
      What Perfect Nations advises
      Rwanda should be the first nation to pilot Standard Tokens, designed by Perfect Nations: small units of finance that citizens, investors and visitors can buy, each tied to one household’s switch to clean cooking, subject to compliance review. With super intelligence, each token can be linked to a verified kitchen, so every buyer sees the family it reached and Rwanda sees its progress home by home.

      Source: World BankRead the Solution

      Every Child Learns

      9 in 10ten-year-olds in Afghanistan who cannot read a simple text

      The challenge
      Nine in ten ten-year-olds in Afghanistan cannot read a simple text.
      What Perfect Nations advises

With super intelligence, lessons in Dari and Pashto can reach any child with a phone or a radio, and learning can be verified without a classroom.
