Design Studio · Perfect Capita

The Angola wheel: held at 25 by food

  • To setPerfect CapitaThe yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards, set with Angola in its own prices.
  • To setThe price of Perfect AngolaPerfect Capita times the population: the national bill, each year.
  • 25CoverageOut of 100, set by food, the standard Angola provides least.
  • ~29mThe GapAngolans below the Living Line for food: three in four are moderately or severely food insecure.

The Living LineThe poverty line measures income. The Living Line measures a life: food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and livelihood. Perfect Capita is the yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards. Coverage scores each standard strictly out of 100, and a nation is held at its weakest: Angola at 25, by food, with education at 42 and health at 44 next.

Perfect Nations estimate, to be confirmed with Angola. The Gap applies FAO’s 2024 food insecurity figure to a population of about 39 million. The strict measures

  • Food25%Not food insecure; 75% are moderately or severely food insecureFAO, 2024
  • Water68%Basic water; safely managed water is not measuredWHO/UNICEF, 2024
  • Shelter55%Outside urban slums; 63% of city dwellers live in slums; safe sanitation is not measuredUN-Habitat, 2022
  • Energy53About 530 kWh a person a year, against 1,000; 56% cook cleanlyIRENA and World Bank, 2022
  • Education4242% complete lower secondary school; reading by age ten is not measuredUNESCO, 2023
  • Health44WHO service coverage indexWHO, 2023
  • Livelihood46%Above US$4.20 a day; 54% live below it; datedWorld Bank, 2018

Strict measures. Food: share not moderately or severely food insecure (FAO). Water: safely managed drinking water where measured; where it is not, basic water, marked as such. Shelter: the lower of safely managed sanitation and the share outside urban slums. Energy: the lower of electricity per person against 1,000 kWh and clean cooking. Education: the lower of children reading by the end of primary school and lower-secondary completion. Health: WHO service coverage index. Livelihood: share above the stricter of the national poverty line and the World Bank line for the nation’s income group. Each figure links to its source.
