Design Studio · Perfect Capita

The India wheel: held at 44 by education

  • To setPerfect CapitaThe yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards, set with India in its own prices.
  • To setThe price of Perfect IndiaPerfect Capita times the population: the national bill, each year.
  • 44CoverageOut of 100, set by education, the standard India provides least.
  • 56%The GapOf Indian children could not read a simple text by the end of primary school in 2017. Education is the guarantee furthest from reach.

The Living LineThe poverty line measures income. The Living Line measures a life: food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and livelihood. Perfect Capita is the yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards. Coverage scores each standard strictly out of 100, and a nation is held at its weakest: India at 44, by education, with shelter at 63 and health at 69 next.

Perfect Nations estimate, to be confirmed with India. The learning figure dates from 2017 and is marked as dated; India does not publish the stricter food-insecurity survey, so food uses a lenient measure. The strict measures

  • Food88%Lenient measure: 12% are undernourished; food insecurity experience is not publishedFAO, 2023
  • Water76%Safely managed drinking waterWHO/UNICEF, 2024
  • Shelter63%Safely managed sanitationWHO/UNICEF, 2024
  • Energy8181% cook cleanly; about 1,400 kWh a person a yearWorld Bank and CEA, 2024
  • Education4444% of children read a simple text by the end of primary school; datedWorld Bank, 2017
  • Health69WHO service coverage indexWHO, 2023
  • Livelihood84%Above US$4.20 a day; 16% live below itWorld Bank, 2023

Strict measures. Food: share not moderately or severely food insecure (FAO). Water: safely managed drinking water where measured; where it is not, basic water, marked as such. Shelter: the lower of safely managed sanitation and the share outside urban slums. Energy: the lower of electricity per person against 1,000 kWh and clean cooking. Education: the lower of children reading by the end of primary school and lower-secondary completion. Health: WHO service coverage index. Livelihood: share above the stricter of the national poverty line and the World Bank line for the nation’s income group. Where a strict measure is not published, a lenient one is shown and marked as such. Each figure links to its source.
