Dear Afghanistan,

Your mountains have stood over the crossroads of Asia for thousands of years. Caravans crossed your passes between China, India and Persia, and the blue lapis of Badakhshan, mined for millennia, reached the treasuries of the ancient world. You have always been a place where the world meets.

Think of what you have already built. You carried water under the desert in karez channels dug by hand, and in the south those channels are flowing again. Your rivers already turn turbines, and power lines from your northern neighbours carry electricity into your valleys; with CASA-1000, that same corridor could carry power on to Pakistan. Your saffron, picked by hand, is sold in stores across America, and Afghan women process it. Your craftspeople have restored or rebuilt 150 historic and community buildings in the old city of Kabul.

Through the hardest years you have kept going, and the world has kept faith with you. Since August 2021, close to $2 billion of World Bank support has reached you through the Afghanistan Resilience Trust Fund and the agencies of the United Nations. Teachers have taught in homes and by satellite, so that girls kept out of secondary school can keep learning. Farmers have planted certified seed bought from local dealers. A nation is not its government, and the nation has not stopped.

The work ahead is hard, and you deserve the truth about it. Your daughters are barred from school after the sixth grade and from university, and while any citizen is barred by law, Perfect Capita cannot be reached. More than nine in ten of your ten-year-olds cannot yet read a simple text. Fewer than one in three of you has safe water at home, and this past winter 17.4 million of you faced a food crisis. None of this is the measure of your people. It is the measure of what has been withheld from them.

Now imagine turning that same endurance toward every home in the country. Picture Perfect Afghanistan: a morning when every daughter walks to school beside her brother, and every girl who wants to become a doctor can become one; when every mother gives birth with a trained midwife beside her and a clinic within reach; when the snowmelt of the Hindu Kush is stored and shared, and no field goes dry. Power from Central Asia runs through your valleys to the clinic, the workshop and the home, and the copper and iron beneath your mountains pay for schools and hospitals. And no Afghan falls below a decent life, because the nation has decided that none will.

None of this needs a miracle. It needs what you have always had: the patience to build what will outlast any season. Your land, your snowmelt, your minerals, your place at the crossroads of Asia and your people are assets no balance sheet counts, and together they can carry every Afghan to food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and a livelihood.

“A nation is not its government. This Letter is written to the farmer, the nurse and the child, and to every daughter who wants to finish school.”

So this is an invitation. Bring your vision, your story and your solutions to the Perfect Afghanistan Design Studio, from home or from abroad, under your own name or a pen name, and join the Co-designers who are already imagining what comes next. Perfect Afghanistan will not be designed for you. It will be designed by you.

Yours in design,

Preeti Sinha

President and CEO, Perfect Nations
