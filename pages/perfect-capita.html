Seven rules keep the measure strict

No averages. No estimates. No one left out.

  1. Strict firstThe strict measure is used wherever it exists. A gentler fallback is labelled as such on the tile.
  2. The five-year ruleData older than five years is shown with its year and marked “dated”.
  3. No data, no numberA missing measure reads “Not measured”, never an estimate.
  4. The legal barWhere a law bars a whole group of citizens from a standard, the tile reads “Barred” and no Coverage score is published: no nation reaches Perfect while any citizen is barred by law.
  5. Every citizenWhere data is split by sex, or by rural and urban, the lower group sets the figure, as data allows.
  6. Strict figures onlyEach tile shows the strict figure and its source. Gentler figures are not shown beside it.
  7. The weakest linkA nation is held at its lowest standard. No nation can make up for failing in health by excelling in education.

The first nation priced

Barbados: $6,204 a person a year.

The price of Perfect Barbados is $1.75 billion a year: about a fifth of the $28,365 Barbados produces per person, for each of 282,623 people. Water, shelter and energy reach almost everyone. Food holds Coverage at 69 of 100, and lifting everyone below the Living Line would cost up to $338 million a year, 4.2 per cent of GDP.
