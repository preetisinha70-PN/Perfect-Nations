01

Pay for results

Money moves only when results are verified.

  • Outcome Bond$225MInvestors earn more as verified results arrive.Brazil · 2024 · Amazon reforestation

    The principal is protected; the return rises with the result, here the carbon a replanted forest removes.

    Source: World Bank, August 2024Brazil blueprint

  • Development Impact Bond160%Investors fund the work; outcome payers repay them for results.India · 2018 · of the learning target

    Investors pay for delivery up front. A donor or foundation repays them, with a return, only once an independent evaluator verifies the results.

    Source: Educate Girls, 2018India blueprint

  • Social Impact BondThe same deal at home, with the government paying for results.Colombia · 2017 · paid for jobs found, not for training given

    Investors fund a programme; the government repays them only for the outcomes it wanted, here formal jobs found and kept.

    Source: Inter-American Development BankColombia blueprint

  • Program-for-Results$588MA loan disbursed only as agreed results are verified.Tanzania · 2021 · for primary school learning

    Each tranche is tied to a disbursement-linked indicator, such as pupils reaching a learning level, and is released when that result is verified.

    Source: World Bank, project P169380Tanzania blueprint

  • Results-Based PaymentsUp to $41.8MA set price for each verified unit of result.Republic of the Congo · 2021 · for 8.4 million tonnes of emissions cut

    A buyer pays a fixed price per verified unit: a tonne of emissions avoided, a child vaccinated, a home connected.

    Source: World Bank, May 2021Republic of the Congo blueprint

02

Labelled sovereign bonds

Public debt with its purpose written into the bond.

  • Social Bond$400MBond money ring-fenced for homes, schools and clinics.Ecuador · 2020 · for affordable homes

    The proceeds can be spent only on eligible social projects, reported to investors each year.

    Source: Inter-American Development Bank, 2020Ecuador blueprint

  • Sustainability-Linked Bond$2BMiss the national climate target and the coupon rises.Chile · 2022 · the first of its kind

    The money is general budget money, but the interest rate steps up if the nation misses targets it set in advance.

    Source: Ministry of Finance of Chile, 2022

  • Sovereign Green BondINR 16,000 crBond money ring-fenced for clean energy and climate.India · 2023 · in the first issuance

    The proceeds finance eligible green projects under a published framework, with allocation reports to investors.

    Source: Reserve Bank of India, 2023India blueprint

  • Sovereign Blue Bond$15MBond money ring-fenced for oceans and fisheries.Seychelles · 2018 · the world’s first

    A green bond for the sea: the proceeds fund marine protection and sustainable fisheries, here with a World Bank guarantee behind it.

    Source: World Bank, October 2018

  • Sovereign Green Sukuk$1.25BGreen bond finance that complies with Islamic law.Indonesia · 2018 · the world’s first

    A sukuk is backed by assets rather than interest; the green sukuk spends every dollar on eligible climate projects.

    Source: UNDP Indonesia, April 2018Indonesia blueprint

  • Sustainable Bond$1.5BOne bond for social and environmental spending together.Brazil · 2025 · orders three times the size

    The proceeds are tied to both social and environmental spending under the nation’s sustainable bond framework.

    Source: National Treasury of Brazil, November 2025Brazil blueprint

03

Debt conversions

Costly debt refinanced; the savings go to people and nature.

  • Debt-for-Nature Swap$150MCheaper debt in exchange for protecting nature.Barbados · 2022 · about 30% of its ocean to be protected

    Guarantors let the nation refinance costly debt; in return it commits the savings and new protected areas to conservation.

    Source: The Nature Conservancy, September 2022Barbados blueprint

  • Debt-for-Climate-Resilience Swap$300MSavings from cheaper debt build climate defences.Barbados · 2024 · the world’s first for resilience

    Multilateral guarantees replace costly debt with cheaper debt, and the savings are spent on resilience: here water and sewerage.

    Source: Inter-American Development Bank, 2024Barbados blueprint

  • Debt-for-Education Swap$400MSavings from cheaper debt build schools.Angola · 2026 · 30 more secondary schools

    A guaranteed commercial loan prepays costlier loans; most of the savings build secondary schools.

    Source: World Bank, March 2026Angola blueprint

  • Debt-for-Development SwapEUR 500MCostly commercial debt bought back; the savings fund education.Côte d’Ivoire · 2024 · about EUR 330M freed for schools

    A World Bank guarantee stands behind a cheaper, longer loan that buys back the most expensive commercial debt.

    Source: World Bank, December 2024Côte d’Ivoire blueprint

04

Guarantees and risk cover

A stronger balance sheet stands behind the deal.

  • Partial Credit GuaranteeUp to $800MA guarantor covers part of the debt, so banks lend more.Tanzania · 2026 · behind a $1.2B railway loan

    A development bank guarantees part of the repayments, which lowers the lenders’ risk and the borrower’s cost.

    Source: African Development Bank, November 2026Tanzania blueprint

  • Payment Guarantee$350MA guarantee on a state buyer’s promise to pay.Bangladesh · 2026 · behind LNG import payments

    Suppliers sell on longer terms because a development bank stands behind the state company’s payments.

    Source: World Bank, May 2026Bangladesh blueprint

  • Infrastructure Guarantee$500MA guarantee that draws private lenders into infrastructure.Mexico · 2025 · for infrastructure and private investment

    The guarantee lets private lenders finance public infrastructure on longer, cheaper terms than they would alone.

    Source: World Bank, December 2025Mexico blueprint

  • Political Risk Insurance$150MCover against expropriation, inconvertibility and unrest.Angola · 2024 · for new water treatment plants

    An insurer pays investors if a named political risk stops the project, so they invest where they otherwise would not.

    Source: U.S. International Development Finance Corporation, December 2024Angola blueprint

  • Small-Business Credit Guarantee$50MGuarantees on small-business loans, so banks lend to them.Barbados · 2025 · up to 80% of each loan

    The facility guarantees up to 80 percent of each loan, capped at $1 million, through local banks.

    Source: Inter-American Development Bank, July 2025Barbados blueprint

  • Local-Currency GuaranteeKSh 4.3BLets a borrower raise money in the currency it earns.Kenya · 2019 · Kenya’s first green bond, repaid in full

    A guarantor backs a bond in local currency, so the borrower carries no exchange-rate risk.

    Source: PIDG, 2024Kenya blueprint

05

Disaster risk finance

Money agreed before the storm, paid within weeks of it.

  • Catastrophe Bond$150MInvestors pay the nation when a disaster crosses a trigger.Jamaica · 2024 · paid in full after Hurricane Melissa

    Investors earn a premium; if a storm or earthquake crosses an agreed threshold, their principal goes to the nation.

    Source: World Bank, November 2025Jamaica blueprint

  • Parametric Risk Pool$483MNations pool disaster risk and pay less for cover.Caribbean · since 2007 · paid out across 82 events

    Member governments buy cover together; payouts follow measured wind, rain or quake readings, with no claims process.

    Source: CCRIF SPCCaribbean blueprint

  • Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option$200MA credit line that opens the day disaster strikes.Colombia · 2026 · after the August earthquake

    The loan is agreed in calm times and drawn the moment a disaster is declared.

    Source: World Bank, September 2026Colombia blueprint

  • Climate-Resilient Debt Clause$500MDebt payments pause for two years after a disaster.Barbados · 2025 · a benchmark-size global bond

    A clause in the bond defers payments after a severe hurricane, flood or epidemic, so the budget can go to recovery.

    Source: Artemis, June 2025Barbados blueprint

06

Blended finance and platforms

Public money placed so private money follows.

  • Blended Finance PlatformR$75BPublic capital and currency cover that bring private investors in.Brazil · 2024 · mobilised in the first year

    The state takes the risks private investors will not, such as currency swings, so they invest in the nation’s priorities.

    Source: COP30 BrazilBrazil blueprint

  • Green Bank$15.5MA national bank that keeps green credit flowing locally.Barbados · 2023 · Green Climate Fund equity

    Public and climate-fund equity capitalise a bank that lends to households and firms for energy, water and resilience.

    Source: Green Climate Fund, FP213Barbados blueprint

  • Resilience and Sustainability Facility$1.038BLong-term IMF money for climate reform.DR Congo · 2025 · beside its core programme

    Affordable, long-maturity IMF financing that sits beside a core programme and rewards climate reforms.

    Source: International Monetary Fund, January 2025DR Congo blueprint

  • Diaspora Bond$300MA bond sold to citizens living abroad.Nigeria · 2017 · Nigeria’s first diaspora bond

    The nation borrows from its own people overseas, who often accept a lower return to invest at home.

    Source: Debt Management Office, NigeriaNigeria blueprint

  • Retail Mobile BondKES 3,000Any citizen can buy a government bond by phone.Kenya · 2017 · to buy in

    A government bond sold in small amounts through mobile money, so ordinary savers can own a share of public finance.

    Source: FSD Kenya, 2017Kenya blueprint

  • Standard TokensA citizen-scale share in one basic living standard.Designed by Perfect Nations · pending compliance

    Designed by Perfect Nations: small units of finance, priced by region, each tied to one of the seven basic living standards. Pending compliance review.

    Source: How Standard Tokens work
