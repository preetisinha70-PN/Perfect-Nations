Design Studio · Perfect Capita

The Barbados wheel: held at 69 by food

  • $6,204Perfect CapitaThe yearly cost, per person, of lifting every Barbadian above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards, in 2025 prices.
  • $1.75bnThe price of Perfect BarbadosPerfect Capita times 282,623 people: the national bill, each year.
  • 69CoverageOut of 100, set by food, the standard Barbados provides least.
  • ~88,000The GapBarbadians below the Living Line for food: about three in ten are moderately or severely food insecure.

The Living LineThe poverty line measures income. The Living Line measures a life: food, water, shelter, energy, education, health and livelihood. Perfect Capita is the yearly cost, per person, of lifting everyone above the Living Line in all seven basic living standards. Coverage scores each standard strictly out of 100, and a nation is held at its weakest: Barbados at 69, by food, with health at 82 and livelihood at 82.5 next.

Perfect Nations estimate in 2025 US dollars, to be confirmed with Barbados. Perfect Capita adds the national poverty lines (Survey of Living Conditions 2016, carried to 2025 prices) to public spending per person on education and health, so nothing is counted twice. The Gap applies FAO’s 2022 food insecurity figure to a population of 282,623. The strict measures

  • Food69%Not food insecure; 31% are moderately or severely food insecureFAO, 2022
  • Water99%Basic water; safely managed water is not measuredWHO/UNICEF, 2024
  • Shelter99.7%Durable dwellings; safe-sanitation data not publishedCensus, 2021
  • Energy100About 4,200 kWh a person a year; all cook cleanlyIRENA and World Bank, 2023
  • Education97Lower-secondary completion; reading data not publishedUNESCO, 2024
  • Health82WHO service coverage indexWHO and UHC2030, 2023
  • Livelihood82.5%Above the national poverty line; the survey is datedIDB and BSS, 2016

The strict measures. Food: share not moderately or severely food insecure (FAO). Water: safely managed drinking water where measured; where it is not, basic water, marked as such. Shelter: the lower of safely managed sanitation and the share outside urban slums. Energy: the lower of electricity per person against 1,000 kWh and clean cooking. Education: the lower of children reading by the end of primary school and lower-secondary completion. Health: WHO service coverage index. Livelihood: share above the stricter of the national poverty line and the World Bank line for the nation’s income group. Each figure links to its source.

The register

  • GovernmentAdvocacyThe Bridgetown InitiativeConvened July 2022 · Version 3.0, September 2024 · Ongoing$650B

    New SDR issuance sought under Bridgetown 3.0

    The Bridgetown Initiative began as a high-level retreat convened in Bridgetown in July 2022, went to the UN General Assembly that September and to COP27 in November. Version 2.0 followed in April 2023 and fed the Paris Summit’s Paris Pact for People and Planet in June 2023 and COP28’s climate finance framework that December. Version 3.0 was released in September 2024 around three pillars: change the rules of the game, shock-proof economies, and dramatically increase financing. It puts the annual climate and nature requirement for emerging and developing economies at $1.8 trillion and the SDG requirement at $1.2 trillion, of which $850 billion must come from external sources. It asks for $500 billion a year in additional official lending, $300 billion a year of thirty to fifty year MDB financing, a $650 billion new SDR allocation, and a minimum $120 billion IDA21 replenishment, alongside levies on aviation, shipping, fossil fuel windfalls and extreme wealth.

    Source
  • GovernmentCompletedComprehensive Debt Restructuring ProgrammeExternal exchange launched 5 November 2019 · Settled from 11 December 2019$774M

    External exchange, after a BBD 11.9bn domestic restructuring in 2018

    The external exchange cut aggregate outstanding principal and past-due and accrued interest by more than 26 percent and issued a 2029 bond. Its natural disaster clause allows Barbados to capitalise interest and defer principal for two years after a qualifying earthquake, tropical cyclone or rainfall event — the drafting the rest of the market has since borrowed.

    Cleary GottliebExternal bondholders

    Source
  • GovernmentCompletedGlobal Bond with Climate Resilient Debt ClausesIssued June 2025 · Priced around 8 percent$500M

    First benchmark-size international bond with climate and pandemic deferral

    Issued in June 2025, this was the first international bond of benchmark size to carry climate and pandemic payment-deferral provisions, buying two years of fiscal breathing room after a severe hurricane, flood or epidemic-scale event. Sebastian Espinosa of White Oak Advisory, which advised Barbados, said the pricing should end the debate over whether markets demand a premium for such clauses.

    White Oak AdvisoryInternational bondholders

    Source
  • GovernmentCompletedDebt-for-Climate-Resilience ConversionClosed December 2024 · Structured as a sustainability-linked loan$300M

    IDB and EIB guarantees of $150m each · savings of about BBD 220m over 10 years

    The world’s first debt-for-climate operation focused on resilience rather than nature. It pairs $300 million of multilateral guarantees with $110 million of upfront funding from the IDB and the Green Climate Fund, including a $40 million GCF grant, and channels the savings into water reclamation, sewerage and water-loss reduction.

In exchange Barbados committed to protect approximately 30 percent of its ocean territory, up from almost none. The marine spatial plan that converts that commitment into gazetted water is due in 2027.
